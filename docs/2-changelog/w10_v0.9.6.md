## Changes

### A novice PlayerBot now runs its own economy loop

F_0.9.5 could mint a novice and train it if XP arrived. F_0.9.6 makes the novice
*earn* that XP unaided: `SimHunterController` gains an early-game state machine
(`LeaveCity → SelectTarget → Engage → Recover → Train`) that leaves the city,
picks a creature it can actually beat, kills it in real combat, rests, trains the
next box, and repeats — with deaths and cloner respawns in between.

Target selection is the same estimate a player makes by eye: a level band around
the bot's own tier (`skillTier ± 3`) plus a time-to-kill window computed from the
equipped weapon's real damage and the target's *minimum* primary HAM pool, via a
new side-effect-free `CombatManager::estimateDamageAfterMitigation`. Server-side
knowledge informs **selection only**, never the fight — the no-cheat invariant
the plan introduced.

### Where a novice hunts: the city's own spawners

The first live runs found the novice standing in empty desert. Mos Eisley's
newbie fauna is not scattered at random — it is placed by `InterestAreaSpawner`
screenplays as `spawn_egg.iff` objects that spawn a pod within ~10 m. The
controller now finds those eggs by zone query around its home city and visits
them in rotation (`roamCitySpawners`, `chooseEarlyGameSpawnerAnchor`), falling
back to the old ring anchors when none is active. In the accepted run the
subject anchored on **eight** different eggs and reached seven.

### Fighting what a novice can survive

Three rules, each added because a live run killed the bot:

- **PACK-only group-aggro filter.** Only `PACK` creatures pull their social group
  (`AiAgent::notifyPackMobs`); `HERD` and a shared social group do not. The first
  cut excluded both and left the novice with no targets at all.
- **Self-defence judges every attacker, not just the target.** A novice was
  engaging a winnable creature while a level-22 womp rat beside it did the
  damage. `findUnwinnableEarlyGameAttacker` re-checks the whole defender list on
  fight entry **and on every Engage tick**, because pack allies join
  asynchronously, and flees the one it cannot beat (`flee_other_attacker`).
- **Heal-on-death, 800 HAM.** Novice bodies get 800 in all nine pools like a
  starting player (`novice.bodyHam`), and clone wounds are healed on respawn
  (`cloneWoundMaxFraction` 0) — the previous 500-point wound pinned a novice's
  HAM at 1. Parity-harness bodies are explicitly exempt.

### The livelock a single-slot memory caused

The first full verification failed at 34/42. Two kreetles sat just off the city
navmesh the novice stood on; every path attempt failed, and the Engage skip
memory held **one** target, so skipping the second erased the skip on the first.
The novice alternated between them for 70 minutes, never engaging, never
re-anchoring, and the hybrid mover logged ~15,300 exit-resolve failures. The fix
is a bounded skip set (`unreachableSkipSeconds` 60, `unreachableSkipMaxTargets`
8, soonest-expiry eviction): once every nearby target is skipped the scan comes
back empty and the existing rotation moves the bot on.

### Observability: two gaps closed

- **Death cause capture.** `AiAgentImplementation::notifyObjectDestructionObservers`
  records the killer into a leaf-locked `SimEarlyGameDeathLog`; `onPveHunterDied`
  is the single finalizer that resolves the site and de-duplicates by body OID.
  Death sites become short-lived avoid zones so the bot stops walking back into
  the pack that just killed it.
- **Kill XP that silently vanishes.** `disseminateExperience` has branches that
  drop a kill with no counter and no log line. While the (default-off) kill-XP
  gate is on, a sim bot in the victim's threat map now bumps
  `killXp.botKillsSeen` and, on a drop, one of `droppedNoDamage` /
  `droppedNoBaseXp` / `droppedNoSimBotAttacker`, plus a `SimPveKillXpDropped`
  line. Counting is lock-free inline; identity resolution and logging ride the
  same deferred-task hand-off the award itself uses.

### F_0.9.6-D1: sim-combat diagnostics

A default-off, allowlist-gated trace (`SimCombatDiagLog` → `bin/log/simcombat.log`,
`combatDiag.logging`) covering behaviour-tree ticks, attack selection/enqueue and
the full kill-XP path (`KILL_XP_CANDIDATE` / `DISSEMINATE` / `AWARD`), with a
`pveActivity.combatDiag` dashboard section. This is what made the kill-XP
accounting assertions checkable rather than inferred.

### Verification

Four live runs over six guarded boots. The accepted run
(`20260915-225334`) passes **43/43**: parity matrix 59+1/60 then 60/60 across a
restart, both kill-XP guards, the traced kill path with every grant
`granted=1`, and a 90-minute production Run D in which the subject novice made
22 paid kills, trained `combat_brawler_unarmed_01` 10 s after hitting its XP
threshold, reached seven spawner anchors, died three times with cause and zone
captured, and never stalled.

Two owner-approved post-hoc contract amendments are recorded in the report and
the contract file: D19 dropped a `defenders >= 2` clause that the behaviour does
not require, and the forced-death probe's `minTtkSeconds` went 600 → 0 (at 600
nothing near the anchors is eligible, so the bot engaged nothing and could not
die — the scaffold, not the code). Also recorded: `core3.log` truncated itself
mid-run, costing two death lines from the log delta; the sampled telemetry
carried the identical lines, so the assertion stands on a second source.

### Gates

Everything ships **default-off**: `playerBotProgression.earlyGameEnabled`,
`earlyGame.realWeaponDamage`, `pveConfig.novice.enabled`,
`playerBotProgression.awardKillXp`, `trainingEnabled`, and `combatDiag.logging`.
The release is inert until the owner turns a gate on.

### Known gaps, deliberately not fixed

- No memory of a creature the bot fled from — it can re-select the same one.
- Target eligibility ignores incoming damage: a level-2 `mountain_worrt` took a
  novice from 572 to 183 HAM in 15 s while reading as an easy kill.
- Flee paths can pin against off-navmesh geometry, which is how packs catch a
  fleeing novice.
- No telemetry for a live body's applied skill mods, so "the bot is using its
  trained box" rests on the four harness body-mod scenarios and the code path.
