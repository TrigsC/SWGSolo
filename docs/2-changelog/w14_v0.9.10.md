## Changes

### Should bots have a real player inventory? No

Before planning, the owner asked for a check that a vault is the right long-term choice,
with two future cases in mind: bots in a player guild, and a player giving a bot a vibro
knuckler. The plan records the reasons for keeping the vault.

- Being a player is decided by template type (`isPlayerCreature()`), not by having a
  `PlayerObject`, so a ghost would not make a bot count as a player.
- Secure trade and guild windows need a client on the bot's side either way.
- Guild membership must be re-applied at spawn in both designs, because a bot's body
  changes ID on every death.
- Gifts already have a stock path (`GiveItemCommand`) that can call the vault's deposit
  function.

### Bot kills produce loot

When a roster PlayerBot gets the kill, the corpse gets stock loot and cash exactly as for a
player (`CreatureManagerImplementation::notifyDestruction`), owned by the bot so a passing
player cannot take it. Gate off, a bot's corpse behaves exactly as before.

### The loot walk

The bot walks to the corpse when no fight is on and loots it: cash goes into its cash
balance in the progression store (journaled), and items go into its bag. On a travel leg
the bot detours to a corpse within 64 m and then resumes the leg. A new attacker, a timeout
(90 s), an expired claim, a vanished corpse or a death ends the walk, and each reason is
counted.

### The hive item vault

- One persistent container owned by `AiEconomyManager`, holding one bag per bot. Moving
  an item into a bag records its owner in the same save, so the bag is the recovery
  source when a record was not yet written.
- `simbot_items` (schema 1015): one row per item, keyed by object ID, with owner, state,
  source and reference. History stays in `simbot_ledger` (`kind = item`).
- Per-bot cap of 80 items. A bot at the cap takes the cash and leaves the items on the
  corpse.
- Item stats are part of the object (for example a component's `attributeMap` and its use
  count). The same object moves, so nothing is copied or re-rolled.
- If the vault cannot be loaded, the loot gate stays closed.

### Recovery and deletion

- At boot every record is checked against the bags. An item whose record was not written
  is recovered from its bag; a held item that is missing becomes `orphaned`, with a −1
  journal row for its bot.
- Deleting a bot (reaper, retirement or harness) destroys its items and its bag first.
- An item looted less than one 5-minute object save before a crash or restart is lost,
  as a player's would be, and is reported as `orphaned`.

### Movement callbacks

The loot walk interrupts other movement, so arrival and path-failure callbacks now carry
the movement generation they were issued under. A callback from replaced movement can no
longer run the interrupted phase's arrival or failure logic.

### Dashboard and harness

- `playerBotProgression.loot` and `.vault` sections, items per bot, and each item's stats
  in the bot's detail drawer. Events `LOOT_CLAIM`, `LOOT_STEP` and `LOOT_RESULT` are
  written to `missiondiag.log`.
- 13 new harness scenarios. `loot_production_walk` spawns a jawa next to a real roster
  bot on a travel leg and checks the whole walk.

## Verification

- 14 of 14 harness scenarios on the final build. Real bot 84 was attacked on its way to a
  mission, killed the jawa, walked to the corpse, looted 65 credits and resumed its leg.
- An item survived a real restart in its owner's bag with identical stats.
- 118 of 118 unit tests, run with the server stopped.
- Live verification found and fixed four defects:
  - the vault's container type check;
  - real bots never looting, because they were always walking;
  - movement-callback races;
  - several harness checks.
- No lootable production corpse appeared in about 3.5 hours of normal play: the lairs
  being worked have empty loot tables, and roster bots only fight back.
