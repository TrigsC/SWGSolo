# Changelog Summary

- **v0.9.14 (Armor loadout, F_0.9.13 P.10n - Week 14, 18-10-2026)**:
  - **Bots wear the armor they own.** A roster bot wears the best usable piece in its vault for each body slot (race, faction and skill rules as for a player), scored by how much damage it absorbs, with a 10 % margin before a swap. The body wears a transient copy; the vault item never moves.
  - **Real protection, real cost.** A flagged bot takes the stock player armor math in combat (hit location, armor rating vs piercing, resists, condition damage); stock NPCs keep their template values. Encumbrance and wearable skill mods apply exactly as for a player.
  - **The player habit.** New armor goes on only while the bot carries the buffs that pay its encumbrance; worn armor stays through buff loss and is put back on after respawn.
  - **Visible, and a face you can recognize.** Bots move to clothing-free bodies across ten species (as the P.7 Jedi), get server-sent clothing that armor swaps off and back on, and keep the same body, features and clothes for life.
  - **Market.** SELL never lists worn armor or the best spare per slot; an optional sub-gate buys a clearly better piece within half the credits above the reserve, one weapon or armor upgrade pending at a time.
  - **Verified live.** 60 of 60 on the final build: 47 harness scenarios, production bots on five species wearing armor bought from the owner's listings and absorbing damage, and the owner's in-client check. Verification found and fixed a config parse-order defect, copies leaking into the object store, and harness buyers taking a real player's listing. Both gates ship off.
- **v0.9.13 (Weapon loadout, F_0.9.12 P.10m - Week 14, 17-10-2026)**:
  - **Bots use what they own.** A roster bot wields the best weapon in its vault that its trained boxes certify it for, of the class its next training box needs (any class once its plan is complete). The body carries a copy with the item's real stats; the item never leaves the vault, so a death cannot lose it. A 10 % margin prevents flip-flopping, and no weapon changes mid-fight.
  - **Kept, not sold.** SELL never lists the equipped weapon or the best usable spare per planned class, and the buy-list keep counts that weapon first, so no duplicates pile up.
  - **Upgrades on the bazaar.** Behind its own sub-gate, a bot instant-buys a certified weapon at least 10 % better, paying at most half its credits above the reserve, through the existing debit-first BUY path.
  - **Verified live.** 34 of 34: all 14 loadout scenarios with the SELL and BUY regressions, and in production bot 274 equipped its vault Vibro Knuckler (130–280) within 41 s of arming and its next kill paid unarmed XP. Live runs found and fixed two product defects (a duplicate kept by two keep rules; hunting bots never re-equipping). Both gates ship off.
- **v0.9.12 (Bazaar BUY, F_0.9.10 P.10k - Week 14, 16-10-2026)**:
  - **Bots buy what their plan needs.** Each training plan can list item templates with a quantity. At its bazaar visit a roster bot instant-buys the cheapest matching listing on the planet, from a player, another bot or the Market Seeder, at or below loot value x markup (or the entry's `maxPrice`), and always keeps a credit reserve. Needed items are never resold.
  - **Debit first, then deliver.** The bot's debit is made durable before the object store moves; the item then goes straight into its vault bag and the seller is paid through the stock path. Boot recovery refunds or commits from evidence (the auction still listed, an item receipt, or the object in the bag). No schema change.
  - **Verified live.** 25 of 25 functional harness scenarios including crash recovery, real bots buying from each other on two boots, and the owner's knuckler bought by a bot for exactly 400. Four product bugs were found and fixed during verification. The gate ships off.
- **v0.9.11 (Bazaar SELL, F_0.9.9 P.10j - Week 14, 16-10-2026)**:
  - **Bots sell their loot on the real bazaar.** A roster bot starting a job walks to an outdoor bazaar terminal and lists vault items at loot value x 3. It never lists below a human listing of the same item, keeps generic junk, and relists an unsold item at its first ask.
  - **The vault bag is the seller.** Six guarded stock branches keep bot listings alive through boot and maintenance and pay the bot instead of resolving a seller by name. Human listings are unaffected.
  - **Instant pickup, durable settlement.** Buyers collect at once. The bot is paid within seconds through ledger receipts covered by an applied mark (schema 1016), and lost sales are recovered at boot.
  - **Verified live.** 33 of 33 and 26 of 26 assertions, real bots listing, and an in-client purchase by the owner that matched to the credit. The gate ships off.
- **v0.9.10 (Loot and the Hive Item Vault, F_0.9.3 P.10d - Week 14, 14-10-2026)**:
  - **Bot kills produce loot.** A roster PlayerBot's kill gets stock loot and cash, owned by the bot; the bot walks to the corpse, even interrupting a travel leg, loots it, and resumes.
  - **The vault is the bot's inventory.** Items move into the bot's own bag in one persistent vault, with one `simbot_items` row each (schema 1015) and a journal row per change; cash goes to the store. Items keep their rolled stats because the same object moves. Cap 80 per bot.
  - **Recovery.** Boot reconciliation recovers unrecorded items from their bag and marks a missing held item `orphaned`; deleting a bot destroys its items and bag. An item looted less than one 5-minute object save before a crash is lost, as for a player.
  - **Ghost spike.** Bots keep the vault rather than a `PlayerObject`: being a player is a template type, and trade and guild windows need a client either way.
  - **Verified live.** Harness 14 of 14, including a real bot looting a jawa mid-mission and an item surviving a restart; four defects found and fixed during verification. The gate ships off.
- **v0.9.9 (Mission Credits and the Holdings Journal, F_0.9.2 P.10c - Week 14, 14-10-2026)**:
  - **Bots are paid for missions.** A roster PlayerBot that destroys its mission lair receives the stock destroy-mission reward (375 x lair level plus up to 15 x level on this server) into its bank balance in the progression store. The formula has one owner, `MissionManager::rollDestroyMissionReward`, which the stock generator now calls too.
  - **Holdings model and journal.** The plan fixes ten rules for anything a bot owns (identity-keyed, nothing durable on the body, one authority and one API per kind, reserve then commit or release). Every credit change writes a row to `simbot_ledger` (schema 1014) in the same flush as the balance; a mismatch after a crash is closed with a visible `reconcile` row.
  - **First credit sink.** A configurable doctor-buff fee is reserved when a buff-up starts and charged when doctor buffs land; a bot that cannot pay goes without them.
  - **Verified live.** 27 of 27 lair completions paid inside the stock range; balances equal journal sums in MySQL for all six bots; 7 fee charges with matching journal rows. Both gates ship off.
- **v0.9.8 (Lair Tactics and Mission Graduation, F_0.9.8 P.10i - Week 14, 13-10-2026)**:
  - **The mission loop completes for the first time.** Overland fallback when recast returns a partial path, a bounded lair-reveal defer, stalled-leg recovery, and a destroyed-lair counter that counts kills. Planned bots graduate to missions at box 4 of one brawler or marksman line.
  - **Lair fights follow the owner's wave rules.** Held engagement (was rebuilt up to 136 times a mission), break off and clear each new wave, shed the previous wave's aggro, keep area specials off the lair, and keep running or fight back during a retreat.
  - **Progression-first dispatch.** A template-incomplete bot is sent to a lair it will fight, ranked for XP at the tier of the weapon it is grinding; market demand only breaks ties.
  - **The walk is survivable more often.** Attackers are recognised from the threat map, a low bot runs toward the mission and rests, and after one flee leg it stands and fights.
  - **Stock-style placement.** The terminal needs only a valid waypoint; the lair point is searched for on arrival (128 m widening to 512 m, 256 tries). Terminal placement failures went from about 80% to none in the release run.
  - **One bug class, four fixes.** A phase transition that books no tick leaves a dead controller, because the timeouts live in the same tick. The proven gates now ship on; mission credits move back to F_0.9.2.

- **v0.9.7 (Skill-Derived Combat Abilities + SimBot Inspection, F_0.9.7 P.10h - Week 10, 19-09-2026)**:
  - **Training now changes how a bot fights.** A PlayerBot's attack list is derived from `simbot_skills` — the same `commands` a real player's boxes grant — and installed on the locked `AiAgent` as a transient overlay beside the SKILLBOX mod overlay. Nothing new persists; the list is re-derived at spawn, at training and on any gate change. Only the highest tier of each chain is installed (`unarmedhit1`/`2` give way to `unarmedhit3`; `unarmedheadhit1` is its own chain).
  - **The probe found the bug before review could.** `skills.iff` spells commands camelCase (`unarmedHit1`), the command registry is lower case, and `getQueueCommand` hashes the exact string — so every hit, lunge, stun and spin was silently discarded as `unknown_command` and only `intimidate1`/`taunt`/`warcry1`/`aim` survived. One `toLowerCase()` took a brawler master from 3 derived abilities to 23.
  - **Specials pay player HAM costs.** `applySpecialAttackCost` returns early for every AiAgent, so stock NPC specials are free; the player arithmetic is now shared and skill-derived agents alone pay it. A reserve stops a special's **own** spend crossing a threshold — and only for pools it actually spends, after the first cut refused free unarmed specials from a wounded bot 186 times and left it dealing no damage.
  - **Three live failures shaped the selection rule.** The scorer is unchanged; skill-derived agents add two pre-filters — the reserve, and any special `CheckAttackIsValid` would reject (lunge is scored as knockdown but carries POSTUREDOWN, so a bot re-picked an invalid lunge for a whole 600 s budget). A free control ability (Intimidate) is used by name *before* the roll with a per-target retry window; checking it only on a missed roll meant a 100 % special policy never used it. `taunt`/`warcry*` ship excluded after a novice spent an entire budget fighting bystanders they pulled.
  - **Seeing what a bot is.** One snapshot builder feeds a Bearer-gated `GET /v1/aieconomy/playerbot/<id>/`, a click-a-name dashboard drawer, and `/server playerinfo` on a targeted SimBot (which used to show the admin their own record). Derivation is gate-independent: with the capability off the view still shows what the boxes *would* grant and why nothing is installed — which is what made the probe possible before anything was enabled.
  - **Verified 27/27** (`20260916-144849`): probe-confirmed tier chains, parity matrix 63 + restart probe then 64/64 with four new ability scenarios, a 47-minute gated grind (68 kills, 46 awards, 23 Intimidates, 139 auto-attacks vs 127 specials, 0 reserve refusals, no superseded ability installed), and two owner-confirmed in-game checks. **Eight defects were found by verification, not review.**
  - **Open item, recorded honestly**: the first matrix attempt hit a SIGSEGV in `AiAgentImplementation::notifyDespawn` on a harness kill-target corpse with a freed `_this`. Two defensive harness fixes ship, but it did not reproduce in 9 boots and ~400 kills — including 6 full-matrix and 3 stress boots with both fixes reverted — so the cause is unconfirmed. It is outside the ability path (the victim was a wild creature), and forensics are preserved in `bin/log`.

- **v0.9.6 (Novice PlayerBot Early-Game Loop, F_0.9.6 P.10g + F_0.9.6-D1 - Week 10, 15-09-2026)**:
  - **A novice now earns its own progression.** `SimHunterController` gains an early-game state machine (`LeaveCity → SelectTarget → Engage → Recover → Train`): leave the city, pick a beatable creature, kill it in real combat, rest, train the next box, repeat. F_0.9.5 could train a bot if XP arrived; this chunk makes the bot produce the XP unaided.
  - **Selection is a player's eye estimate, never a combat advantage.** A level band around the bot's own tier (`skillTier ± 3`) plus a TTK window from real weapon damage and the target's *minimum* primary pool, via the new side-effect-free `CombatManager::estimateDamageAfterMitigation`. Server-side knowledge informs selection only — the plan's no-cheat invariant.
  - **Hunt where the game puts the fauna.** Newbie creatures are placed by `InterestAreaSpawner` screenplays as `spawn_egg.iff` objects; the controller finds those eggs by zone query and visits them in rotation, falling back to ring anchors. The accepted run anchored on eight eggs and reached seven.
  - **Three survival rules, each paid for by a dead bot.** Only `PACK` creatures pull their social group (`HERD` does not — the first cut excluded both and left zero targets); self-defence judges **every** defender on entry *and* every Engage tick, because pack allies join asynchronously; and novice bodies carry 800 HAM in all nine pools with clone wounds healed on respawn (the old 500-point wound pinned HAM at 1).
  - **A single-slot memory is a bug when the world offers two of the thing.** Verification failed 34/42 on a 70-minute livelock: two kreetles just off the city navmesh, one skip slot, so skipping the second erased the first. Fixed with a bounded skip set (8 entries, 60 s, soonest-expiry eviction) — once everything nearby is skipped the scan comes back empty and the existing rotation moves the bot on.
  - **Silent XP loss is now counted.** `disseminateExperience` could drop a kill with no counter and no log; while the default-off kill-XP gate is on, a sim bot in the victim's threat map bumps `killXp.botKillsSeen` and, on a drop, one of three `dropped*` counters plus a `SimPveKillXpDropped` line — counted lock-free inline, logged on the same deferred hand-off the award uses.
  - **F_0.9.6-D1** adds the allowlist-gated `SimCombatDiagLog` (`bin/log/simcombat.log`, `combatDiag.logging`) covering tree ticks, attack enqueue and the whole kill-XP path, plus a `pveActivity.combatDiag` dashboard section — which is what made the kill-XP assertions checkable instead of inferred.
  - **Verified 43/43** over six guarded boots (`20260915-225334`): 22 paid kills, training 10 s after the XP threshold, seven spawner anchors, three deaths with cause and zone captured, no stall. Two owner-approved post-hoc amendments are on the record: a D19 clause the behaviour does not require, and a forced-death probe whose 600 s minimum fight length made the bot engage nothing and therefore unable to die — the scaffold, not the code.
  - **Recorded honestly**: `core3.log` truncated itself mid-run and cost two death lines from the log delta (the sampled telemetry carried the identical lines); and four behaviours ship knowingly unfixed — no memory of a fled creature, no incoming-damage term in eligibility, flee paths that pin against off-navmesh geometry, and no telemetry for a live body's applied skill mods.

- **v0.9.5 (Skill Training and Novice PlayerBots, F_0.9.5 P.10f - Week 8, 06-09-2026)**:
  - **Earned XP becomes progression.** `trainPlayerBotSkill` is the single entry point and reads skill data straight from `SkillManager`, because `awardSkill` rejects every ghostless creature. Prerequisites are checked against `simbot_skills` using `getSkillsRequired()`, never `fulfillsSkillPrerequisites`.
  - **Derived spend makes training a one-row mutation.** `simbot_experience` means *lifetime earned* — already what F_0.9.1 wrote — so available XP is `earned − Σ trained-box cost` and `skill_points_spent` is recomputed from `simbot_skills`. Training writes exactly one `simbot_skills` row: atomic under MyISAM, self-healing at boot, **no write-ahead journal needed**. A journal remains the right tool for F_0.9.6's bazaar, where value is human-visible and multi-party.
  - **The ladder derives itself from `skills.iff`.** Lua names only the goal box; C++ walks the prerequisite closure and topologically orders it, resolving the brawler to **23 boxes / 92 points**. No grind-guide research was required — the graph, costs and mods are already in `skillMap`. Marksman and artisan are one Lua line each.
  - **One skill mod, two opposite treatments, both deliberate.** The combat tier sums **trained deltas only**, because a real player has no template statistic behind `private_<weapon>_combat_difficulty`. The body overlay **must** add `npcTemplate->getStatistic(mod)`, because `AiAgent::getSkillMod` returns the creature-list value whenever nonzero and only then consults the template. Unifying these is a bug in either direction.
  - **Two corrections to shipped F_0.9.1 behaviour**: the lifetime ceiling now follows the stock max-`getXpCap()` rule rather than only `getDefaultXpLimit`; and the per-kill cap tier derives from trained mods via the stock `min(25, difficulty/100 + 1)` formula instead of a `skillTier` frozen at mint. Without both, a trained master would have kept earning tier-1 XP against a stranded ceiling.
  - **`pveMaxHunters` was never a population cap.** The old governor skipped identities that already had bodies *before* counting them, so it bounded a spawn batch. It is now a desired-active-set reconciler: compute the set, drain at most one out-of-set body per tick via the `drainSimPresenceBodies` choreography, then spawn at most `cap − liveCount` **recounted after the drain**.
  - **Ownership had to become explicit.** A governor that only *adds* bodies never has to know which it owns; adding a drain made that load-bearing, and it destroyed the parity harness's own bodies. Ownership is now `isGovernorManagedIdentity`, deliberately independent of runtime-refreshable eligibility, and eligibility is computed **once** into a set both selection passes filter to. Four review findings in this one function all traced to the same shape: a predicate written in more than one place, then drifting.
  - **Live verification took 17 runs and found what nothing else could.** Two product defects were invisible to a warning-clean build, unit tests and code review because they only appear under the shipped default-off configuration: the training gate checked its capability flag *before* the parity override, making training unverifiable by construction; and the governor drained bodies it did not own — caught only by a **pre-existing F_0.9.1 scenario** that assumed a body would still exist.
  - **Recorded honestly**: one mid-verification stall (141 % CPU, REST dead ~30 min) was never explained and did not recur; the "2000 identity" scale scenarios are arithmetic bounds checks, **not** load tests; and the parity matrix's order-dependent global state produced six separate failures and wants per-scenario isolation as its own chunk.

- **v0.9.1 (Kill XP for Roster PlayerBots, F_0.9.1 P.10b - Week 8, 03-09-2026)**:
  - **The first production caller of F_0.9.0's award API**, and the first real economy mutation in the P.10 line. Roster bots now earn combat XP for what they kill, using the engine's own player arithmetic.
  - **No combat-side change was needed**: `ThreatMap::addDamage` already records *every* attacker, AiAgents included, keyed by that attacker's weapon `getXpType()`. Bot hunters were always in the victim's threat map — the data existed and nothing collected it.
  - **Hook site corrected from the roadmap**: the branch went *inside* `PlayerManagerImplementation::disseminateExperience`, not at the `CreatureManagerImplementation.cpp:677` call site. That function has two callers (single kill, and `DisseminateExperienceTask` for lairs), so hooking inside covers both with one branch and reuses the already-computed `baseXp`/`totalDamage`/`gcwBonus`. The roadmap records the correction so later chunks inherit it.
  - **Zero locks on the death path**: the corpse is already locked when `disseminateExperience` runs, so the branch only copies a POD and defers to `SimPlayerBotKillXpTask`, which resolves under `pveMutex` then writes under `progressionMutex` — never nested, never holding an agent lock, never capturing a reference to the corpse.
  - **`getSimPlayerBot()` is a cost filter, never authority** — that is the flag that once leaked onto wild creatures (`d38877020c`). A stale true costs one task that resolves nothing; a stale false costs one kill's XP. Live verification watched 29 wildlife deaths reach the task and get rejected as non-roster, which is the fail-closed design working on production traffic.
  - **Parity includes the rounding**: the direct path truncates *twice* (at `awardExperience`'s `int amount` boundary, then again after `globalExpMultiplier`) while `combat_general` truncates once because `combatXp` is already a `uint32`. Bot level comes from `skillTier`, never the body template — a wraith-templated body would otherwise cap at 178x300. `killXpRate` (default `1.0`) applies *after* the per-kill cap so the knob is not swallowed by it.
  - **The lifetime ceiling moved into `grantPlayerBotExperience`** so every future XP source inherits it instead of re-implementing it; a type already at its ceiling returns `false` with no rejection counter, because the caller passed a valid amount and the store simply had no room.
  - **Scenario 28 is the call-site proof and needed no controller change**: harness and hunter identities share one body-creation path (rifle, faction 0, `ATTACKABLE`), and `simParityTest` overrides only `IDLE` — the stock combat sockets were live all along. A bot killed a spawned `dwarf_nuna` and banked the exact oracle award, durable across flush+reload and a real process restart.
