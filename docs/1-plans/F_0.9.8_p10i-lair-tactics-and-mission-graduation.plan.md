# F_0.9.8 — Lair Tactics and Mission Graduation (P.10i) Implementation Plan

## Overview

A trained PlayerBot can already fight (F_0.9.7) and already earns its own XP
(F_0.9.6), but it stops at lone creatures. This chunk graduates it to **destroy
missions**: take an offer at a real mission terminal, walk to the advertised
waypoint, trigger the lair at the proximity check, clear all waves plus any boss,
destroy the lair, get **paid**, and then decide what to do next — rebuff, run the
second held offer, train, or go back for a fresh pair.

Two tactics are supported and chosen per mission by a computed survivability
estimate: **pull discipline** for a bot that cannot tank a full field, and
**stand-and-AoE** for one that can. The credit half of F_0.9.2 (P.10c, never
implemented) is folded in as Phase 1, because a mission that pays nothing cannot
demonstrate the "do I need buffs?" decision this chunk exists to add.

All new tactical and credit behaviour ships behind
`playerBotProgression.lairTacticsEnabled` and
`playerBotProgression.awardMissionCredits`, both default-off. **One deliberate
exception**: the `HUNTING` phase timeout (Phase 0) is ungated but scoped to
mission orders — an unbounded phase is a defect on its own evidence, and gating
a safety bound behind the feature it protects would leave it live in the
shipped default. See Phase 0 for the reasoning and the stricter alternative.

## Problem Statement

### What already works (and must not be rebuilt)

The F_0.5.0 / P.8.7 market-driven mission loop already delivers most of the
mechanical path the owner described. Verified in this tree:

| Owner's requirement | Already implemented | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Go to a destroy mission terminal | Real terminal resolve + visit, epoch-guarded board handoff | `SimHunterController` mission phases; `SimPlayerManager::openTerminalVisit`, `generatePveBotMissionOffers` |
| Hold 2 missions | `missionBoard.maxHeldMissions = 2`, offer chaining on completion | `sim_player_manager.lua:2234`; `SimHunterController::beginNextMissionOffer` |
| Lair does not spawn on accept; spawns on proximity | `missionBoard.lairRevealRadiusMeters = 120` → `revealPveBotMissionLair` builds a real `LairObject` + `DestroyMissionLairObserver` and fires wave 1 | `SimPlayerManager.cpp:19461-19752` |
| Kill the field before hitting the lair | Works as written, and target selection is already restricted to lair members (`targetMatchesSpecies:3882-3889`) — but the deliberate engage path is used in only 10 of 48 reached lairs; see below | `SimHunterController.cpp:3794-3813` |
| Lair destruction completes the mission | Offer marked `completed`, `pveMissionsCompletedTotal++` | `SimPlayerManager.cpp:19376-19398` |

### The mission loop rarely completes (owner field report — confirmed, cause NOT yet established)

The owner reports that PvE hunters rarely completed missions, abandoned often,
and usually killed only a few first-wave creatures. **The outcome is confirmed.
My first attempt at a root cause was wrong, and is recorded here because the
wrong answer is instructive.**

**What the evidence actually shows.** `bin/log/missiondiag.log` (2026-07-28, the
last run with real mission traffic; live counters are zero because the roster is
idle). Counts are from the window after the `LAIR_ATTACK` probe was added
(`t >= 1785185096477`) — that diagnostic did not exist for the log's first 22
hours, and the naive whole-file ratio overstates the effect.

**Read the funnel carefully; two denominators are easy to confuse.**
`OFFERS_ACCEPT` is **one board visit** carrying `have` offers
(`have=2 want=2`, `SimHunterController.cpp:1503-1544`), not one accepted
mission. The window's 137 accept events therefore represent ~271 held offers,
while `LAIR_REACHED` counts individual missions. Board-level and mission-level
numbers are listed separately for that reason.

| Event | Count | Level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `OFFERS_ACCEPT` | 137 (~271 offers) | board visits |
| `LAIR_REACHED` | 48 | missions |
| `LAIR_ENGAGE` / `LAIR_ATTACK` | 10 / 10 | missions |
| `LAIR_DESTROYED_CREDIT` | 21 | missions completed |
| `MISSION_CLEANUP` | 107 | 101 pre-lair, **6 lair-phase** |

**The lair-phase abandons are small but real, and they match the owner's
description.** Filtering cleanups on `lairAlive=true` (the whole log carries 8;
6 fall in the window):

| Reason / phase | Count | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `phase_timeout_RETREATING` / `RETREATING` | 3 | retreated mid-fight and never resumed, until the 180 s phase timeout |
| `travel_home` / `HUNTING` | 4 | gave up mid-fight and walked home |
| `hunter_died` / `HEALING` | 1 | died at the lair |

The three `phase_timeout_RETREATING` events are the signature worth noting:
`checkMissionSocialAggro`'s mission branch does exactly this — `setPhase(RETREATING)`,
disengage, move away — and `RETREATING` carries a 180 s timeout. A bot that
retreats from a group it cannot resume against, then times out, **is** "killed a
few creatures in the first wave and abandoned". This upgrades the adds-cap
defect below from a latent hazard to a **probable contributor**, though 6 events
is too small a sample to call it the dominant cause.

Conclusions, and the open question:

1. **The dominant loss is still before the lair.** 101 of 107 window cleanups
   are pre-lair, 87 of them `mission_offer_path_failed` — the bot cannot walk to
   the mission location. Lair tactics cannot improve that number.
2. **Lair-phase abandons exist** (6 in window) and are concentrated in
   retreat-without-resume and give-up-and-go-home.
3. **Lairs die more often than they are explicitly engaged** — 21 completions
   against 10 `LAIR_ENGAGE` events. Something other than the deliberate
   "field clear → attack lair" path destroys lairs; AoE splash while fighting
   members is the leading hypothesis, and the lair's own spawn self-damage is a
   second. Phase 0 instruments this rather than assuming it.
4. **Open: why only 10 of 48 reached lairs are explicitly engaged.** Real, and
   its cause is **not established**.

**A root cause I asserted and then disproved.** I first concluded that the
engage gate never opens because `scanForTarget` is a plain radius query with no
lair-membership notion, so unrelated wildlife keeps the field non-empty. **That
is wrong.** `targetMatchesSpecies` (`SimHunterController.cpp:3882-3889`), applied
by `scanForTarget` at `:4025`, already requires a mission candidate's
`getHomeObject()` to equal `missionLairOid`. Mission target selection *is*
restricted to lair members. The 122/10 `foundTarget` split therefore means live
**lair members** were repeatedly found — the field was genuinely occupied — not
that the bot was distracted by passing fauna. Any Phase 0 built on the wildlife
theory would have been a fix for a defect that does not exist.

One other stale belief corrected while checking: the in-code comment at `:3794`
attributes a non-clearing field to wave mobs respawning.
`LairObserverImplementation::checkRespawn` returns early for
`isDestroyMissionLairObserver()` (`:590-614`), so **destroy-mission lairs never
respawn mobiles**. Whatever keeps the field occupied, it is not respawns.

**Two real defects found while investigating**, independent of the open question
and worth fixing on their own evidence:

- **`HUNTING` has no phase timeout.** Every other phase has one
  (`:3336-3356`); `HUNTING` is absent, so a bot that cannot finish a field
  churns until the offer ages out at 1800 s with no attributable end.
- **`checkMissionSocialAggro` measures the wrong thing for a lair fight.**
  `adds` is the hunter's **entire** defender list (`:1685-1687`) against
  `pveMissionMaxSimultaneousAdds = 3` for 8 cycles, and its retreat branch calls
  `disengageTarget(false)`, which removes only the **target** as a defender
  (`:4473-4474`) — so the cohort that put it over the cap survives the retreat
  meant to clear it. A lair fight is a group fight by definition; a rule written
  for lone wilderness pulls treats the intended situation as an anomaly. The
  three `phase_timeout_RETREATING` cleanups above carry its exact signature, so
  it is a probable contributor today and a certain hazard the moment this chunk
  makes bots stand in lair fights deliberately.

**Consequence for this plan.** Phase 0 is therefore **diagnosis first**, not a
fix for a guessed cause — the same ordering that made F_0.9.7 work, where the
gate-independent inspection endpoint found the camelCase defect before anything
was enabled. It ships the two independently-justified fixes above and the
instrumentation needed to name the real cause, and the tactic layer commits to a
remedy only after the probe reports. This is the "mission defects surface for
the first time" risk the roadmap §5c.4 flagged, arriving where predicted.

### What is actually missing

1. **No tactics.** The bot clears whatever `scanForTarget` returns, then walks
   into weapon range of the lair and attacks it. There is no notion of pulling
   one creature at a time, of a wave boundary, or of withdrawing.
2. **No survivability judgement.** Nothing compares the incoming damage of a
   full field against the bot's pools, armour and buffs.
3. **No graduation.** The F_0.9.6 arbiter (`scorePlayerBotActivity`,
   `SimPlayerManager.cpp:11375`) knows exactly two activities, `grind` and
   `hunt`, and `hunt` is the **demand-gated** market loop. A graduated novice on
   a saturated market gets no order and idles — the precise deadlock §5c.1 of the
   roadmap documents.
4. **No offer filtering by danger.** Offers are selected on difficulty band and
   yield family only. A novice can be handed a ranged-creature lair.
5. **Missions pay nothing.** F_0.9.2 was never implemented; there is no
   `awardMissionCredits` gate and no caller of `grantPlayerBotCredits`
   (`SimPlayerManager.h:3383`, shipped with the F_0.9.0 API and no production
   caller).
6. **No "what next".** On lair destruction the controller chains to the next
   offer or falls back to the terminal. Buffs, HAM state, training affordability
   and death history are not consulted.

### Correction to the roadmap's tactical premise — read this before designing

Roadmap §5c.3 states that **"attacking the lair aggros every living creature at
once (`doAggro(lair, attacker, allAttack)`)"**. That is **not what the code does
for destroy-mission lairs**, and the whole low-tier tactic depends on the
difference. Established by reading `LairObserverImplementation.cpp`,
`DestroyMissionLairObserverImplementation.cpp` and `LairAggroTask.h`:

- `doAggro` has exactly one caller: `LairAggroTask::run`.
- `LairAggroTask` is constructed in three places. Two pass
  `allAgentsAttack = false` (`LairObserverImplementation.cpp:422`,
  `DestroyMissionLairObserverImplementation.cpp:73`). Only
  `checkForBossSpawn` (`LairObserverImplementation.cpp:583`) passes `true`, and
  `DestroyMissionLairObserver` does **not** override `checkForBossSpawn`.
- With `allAttack == false`, `doAggro` rolls `System::random(100) < 50` per
  creature and skips on a hit — so roughly **half** the field aggros, not all.
- The task is scheduled `AGGRO_TASK_DELAY * 1000` = **4 seconds** after the
  damaging hit, and is rate-limited by `lastAggroTime` to once per
  `AGGRO_CHECK_INTERVAL` = **30 seconds**.

So the real mechanic is: *damaging the lair schedules a delayed, partial aggro
pull 4 seconds later, at most once per 30 seconds.*

**Critically — `doAggro` applies no distance or aggro-radius test.** It iterates
`spawnedCreatures` and calls `addDefender(attacker)` directly
(`LairObserverImplementation.cpp:272-295`). **Withdrawing cannot dodge the
pull.** Any design that treats the 4-second delay as an escape window is wrong.
Four consequences shape the tactic instead:

- **The partial pull is unavoidable and must be budgeted, not dodged.** Each
  lair-damage window costs at most one pull of ~50 % of the *then-living* field.
- **The lever is the size of that field, not the bot's position.** `doAggro`
  skips creatures that are dead or out of the zone, so 50 % of an empty field is
  zero. Clearing the field before touching the lair makes the pull free — which
  is what `lairEngageAfterFieldClear` was already reaching for; what is missing
  is a *wave-settle* rule so "clear" is true rather than merely observed.
- **Withdrawal earns a smaller place than avoidance, and the plan claims only
  that.** `addDefender` is applied to the whole aggroed cohort at once;
  distance only means the creatures must path to the bot, which **spreads their
  arrivals** over travel time. It does **not** guarantee one-at-a-time combat —
  similar-speed creatures at similar distances arrive together. So withdrawal is
  a mitigation that reduces peak concurrency, not a mechanism that serialises
  the fight, and **viability is priced against the simultaneous cohort**
  (§2), never against a single creature.
- **Damage the lair in concentrated bursts.** Because the pull is rate-limited
  to once per 30 s, the second and subsequent hits inside a window are free.
  Chip damage spread across windows pays the pull repeatedly for no gain.

Two further facts follow from where the aggro task is scheduled:

- At `spawnNumber >= 3`, `notifyObserverEvent`'s DAMAGERECEIVED branch stops
  scheduling `checkForNewSpawns` altogether, so **the partial pull cannot fire
  again**. The final push on the lair is free of it.
- **The boss pull is the dangerous one and is categorically different**:
  `checkForBossSpawn` is the only `allAttack = true` path, it fires at
  `spawnNumber >= 3` on a `CREATURE` lair with `hasBossMobs()`, and it pulls
  **100 %** of the living field with no roll. Pull discipline cannot mitigate
  it — only survivability or avoidance can.

Three further mechanics matter and are not in the roadmap:

- **Wave boundaries are condition-gated, not time-gated.**
  `DestroyMissionLairObserverImplementation::checkForNewSpawns` increments
  `spawnNumber` 0→1 at reveal (`forceSpawn`), 1→2 when `conditionDamage > 0`,
  and 2→3 when `conditionDamage > maxCondition / 2`.
- **The lair damages itself as it spawns.** For `spawnNumber >= 2`,
  `spawnLairMobile` ends with `lair->inflictDamage(lair, ...)`. A wave can
  therefore cascade the next boundary with no bot input — the tactic must never
  assume the bot is the only damage source.
- **Creatures heal the lair** every `HEAL_CHECK_INTERVAL` = 15 s while it has
  condition damage (`checkForHeal`). A pull-discipline bot that is too slow
  loses ground on the lair between waves.

Mission-lair `maxCondition` is set by our own reveal code as
`difficultyLevel * (900 + random(200))` (`SimPlayerManager.cpp:19667`).

## Solution Architecture

Five pieces, layered so each is independently gate-able and independently
observable:

1. **Mission credits (folded F_0.9.2).** A deferred credit grant at the existing
   completion site, using the real `MissionManager` destroy-mission reward
   formula, idempotent per `offerId`.
2. **A survivability estimate** — a pure, side-effect-free function that scores a
   lair's expected field against the bot's current pools/armour/buffs and returns
   a tactic plus the numbers behind it.
3. **A lair tactic sub-state machine** on `SimHunterController`, entered when the
   lair is revealed and exited when it is destroyed or the mission is abandoned.
   Two tactics share one machine; they differ in the transition rules, not in the
   states.
4. **Offer danger filtering** at generation time, so a low-tier bot is never
   handed a ranged or aggressive-pack lair.
5. **The arbiter gains a `mission` activity**, plus a post-mission "what next"
   decision (buff → chain → train → new pair) and the first **credit sink**:
   paying a buffer.

The lair tactic layer makes **no behavioural change** to the engine's lair
machinery. It observes `LairObserver::getSpawnNumber()`, the new
`getBossesSpawned()` accessor and the world; it never calls `checkForNewSpawns`,
`doAggro` or `spawnLairMobile`. The single edit to `LairObserver.idl` is one
additive read-only getter (§3); `DestroyMissionLairObserver` is untouched.

## Implementation Details

### 0. Diagnose the mission loop, and fix what is already proven (prerequisite)

**Files**: `SimHunterController.{h,cpp}`, `SimPlayerManager.{h,cpp}`,
`MissionDiagLog.h` usage, `sim_player_manager.lua`

Two independently-justified fixes, plus the instrumentation that answers the
open question before the tactic layer assumes an answer.

**Fixes justified by the evidence already in hand:**

- **Bound `HUNTING`.** Add it to the phase-timeout switch
  (`SimHunterController.cpp:3336-3356`) as `missionHuntTimeoutMs`, default 600 s,
  applied only to mission orders (`missionHuntOrder`) so legacy demand hunts keep
  their current unbounded behaviour. **This one is deliberately not behind
  `lairTacticsEnabled`**, and the Overview's "everything is gated" claim is
  narrowed accordingly: an unbounded phase is a defect on its own evidence, the
  gate exists to make *new tactical behaviour* reversible, and gating a safety
  bound behind the feature it protects would leave the defect live in exactly the
  default configuration the server ships. It is still bounded to mission orders,
  so no demand hunt changes. If the owner prefers strict gate-off parity, the
  alternative is to gate it and accept that shipped-default missions keep
  drifting to offer expiry.
- **Scope the adds cap out of gated lair fights.** While a mission lair is live
  **and** `lairTacticsEnabled` is on, the tactic layer's survivability check is
  the abandon authority, not `checkMissionSocialAggro`. Gate-off is
  byte-for-byte legacy. **Independently and ungated**, count `adds` as
  **non-member attackers only** while a mission lair is live: lair members are
  excluded entirely. Counting members "but correctly" does not help — a wave is
  2–5 creatures against a cap of 3, so a four-member wave still trips it for its
  8 cycles and still abandons in the shipped default, which is the symptom the
  owner reported. The cap's legitimate job on a mission is to notice *unrelated*
  attackers piling on; the lair group is the assignment. See Backward
  Compatibility, where this is recorded as an intentional gate-off change.

**Instrumentation to name the real cause** (default-off, run-scoped, under the
existing `MissionDiagLog`):

- Extend `LAIR_FIELD` from a bare `foundTarget` boolean to the facts that
  discriminate the hypotheses: the chosen target OID, its `getHomeObject()` OID,
  the live lair-member count from `waveObserver->getSpawnedCreatures()`, the
  observer's `getSpawnNumber()`, and the bot's HAM fraction. The current boolean
  cannot distinguish "field genuinely full", "same creature re-acquired every
  tick", "creature unkillable", or "bot too weak", and those need different
  remedies.
- Add `LAIR_MEMBER_KILL` per lair-member death, and a per-offer summary at
  mission end: members spawned, members killed, time in `HUNTING`, and why the
  mission ended.
- **Attribute every lair-damage event**, not an aggregate. An aggregate cannot
  separate the three candidate sources, and they imply different remedies:
  record attacker OID, whether the attacker is the bot, the bot's current
  sub-phase/target, and the `attacker == lair` case that marks the documented
  spawn self-damage. Only this distinguishes deliberate attack from AoE splash
  from the lair damaging itself — the hypothesis behind conclusion 3.
- Record **why the bot entered `RETREATING`** (social-adds cap, HAM threshold, or
  tactic withdrawal), so the `phase_timeout_RETREATING` signature above becomes
  attributable instead of inferred.
- Publish `lairMembersAlive` and `nonMemberEngagements` on the inspect drawer.

**Phase 0 has an explicit hold point.** Phases 1, 2, 4 and 5 are independent of
the open question and may proceed in parallel. **Phase 3 (the tactic state
machine) does not start until Phase 0's instrumentation has run live and the
plan has been amended with what it found** — that is the F_0.9.7 ordering
(read-only view first, probe, then build), and it is the difference between
fixing the cause and shipping a remedy for a guessed one. If the probe shows the
engage ratio is explained by something Phase 3 already handles, the amendment
records that and Phase 3 proceeds unchanged.

**Acceptance for this phase is a diagnosis, not a ratio.** The honest
before-number is 10 explicit engages per 48 reached lairs, with 21 completions —
so the deliberate path is under-used but the outcome is not zero. The probe must
say *why* before Phase 3 commits to a remedy, and the plan's later phases assume
only that "the field must be clear of **lair members**", which the code already
enforces through `targetMatchesSpecies` and which the estimator (§2) reads from
the observer rather than a radius scan.

**Note on the dominant failure, which this chunk does not address.**
`mission_offer_path_failed` and `mission_offers_unavailable` account for the
large majority of mission loss (87 and 558 events respectively; no meaningful
percentage can be quoted until mission *starts* have their own counter, since
`OFFERS_ACCEPT` counts board visits). They are **out of scope** for this chunk — they are travel and offer-generation defects, not lair tactics.
They are recorded here so the release is not mistaken for a fix to the mission
system's largest failure mode, and they are the strongest candidate for the
chunk after this one.

### 2. Survivability estimate and tactic selection

**Files**: `SimPlayerManager.{h,cpp}` (the estimator, manager-side so the harness
can call it directly), new header
`src/server/zone/objects/creature/simplayer/SimBotLairTactics.h`

**Measure the live field; extrapolate only what has not spawned yet.**
`CombatManager::estimateDamageAfterMitigation` requires a **live
`WeaponObject`**, which a `CreatureTemplate` cannot provide, so the estimate
cannot be built from template data alone. It does not need to be: wave 1 is
already in the world when the bot arrives (the reveal calls
`checkForNewSpawns(..., forceSpawn = true)`), so the bot can measure the real
thing.

**Exact input resolution** (`evaluateLairTactic(identityId, bodyOid, lairOid)`):

- **Observed field** — snapshot `waveObserver->getSpawnedCreatures()`
  (`SpawnObserver.idl:67`, a `SynchronizedVector<CreatureObject>`) and keep the
  live, in-zone entries. This is deliberately **not** a zone range query around
  the lair: a range query omits exactly the creatures that matter most — the ones
  already pathing toward a withdrawn bot — and the observer's own list is the
  authority on lair membership. For each entry:
  `CreatureObject::getWeapon()` (`CreatureObject.idl:1822`) gives
  the real `WeaponObject`; raw damage is the midpoint of the weapon's min/max;
  the attack period is the weapon's attack speed. Per-creature incoming DPS is
  `estimateDamageAfterMitigation(botAgent, creatureWeapon, rawDamage) * hitRate / attackPeriod`,
  called with the **bot as defender** so its armour and active buffs are
  reflected. `hitRate` is `earlyGame.assumedHitRate`, reused rather than
  re-derived.
- **Unspawned waves** — count only, from the same arithmetic
  `DestroyMissionLairObserverImplementation::checkForNewSpawns` uses
  (`spawnLimit = getSpawnLimit() + (difficulty == 0 ? -3 : difficulty == 4 ? +3 : 0)`;
  creature lairs spawn `spawnLimit / 3` per wave, minimum 2). Their damage is
  assumed equal to the **cached first-complete-wave mean**, since a lair's waves
  draw from one `getWeightedMobiles()` list. Reproducing the observer's own
  numbers rather than inventing a second wave-size rule is deliberate: a
  duplicated predicate that drifts is the defect class F_0.9.5 §(4) records.
- **The sample must be valid, and at a boundary it often is not.** The counter
  advances *before* the new creatures exist, and the previous field has just been
  cleared — so a reassessment fired at the raw boundary can see an empty or
  half-spawned list and would compute a reassuringly small number out of nothing.
  Three rules close this: reassessment happens **after** `LAIR_WAVE_SETTLE`
  reports arrivals complete, never at the raw increment; the per-creature mean
  from the **first wave observed complete** is cached on the lair record and
  reused for extrapolation; and when no valid sample exists (no cached mean *and*
  a live count below the expected wave size) the verdict is **`unviable`**, not
  an optimistic estimate. A chooser that cannot see must not clear the bot to
  fight — the same fail-closed posture F_0.8.0 gave its harness exit assertion
  (its *mover* fails open, because a mover that cannot see must still move).
- **Aggregation is cumulative, not per-wave.** Creatures persist until killed, so
  the estimate sums the live field plus every wave that will spawn before the
  lair dies, decremented by the bot's own kill rate — not the size of one wave.
- **Outgoing damage and TTK** reuse the F_0.9.6 helper, which is today
  controller-local and target-object-based. It is **extracted to a shared
  manager-side function taking (attacker, weapon, defender)** so the estimator
  and the existing early-game selector call one implementation. Extraction is
  part of Phase 2, not an incidental edit.
- **Boss** — if the lair is `LairTemplate::CREATURE` and `hasBossMobs()`, price
  the boss wave *and* the `allAttack = true` 100 % pull that comes with it.

**Two incoming-DPS figures, not one.** AoE survival and pull survival are
measured against different cohorts, so a single scalar cannot express both. The
estimator resolves and passes **both**: `fullFieldIncomingDps` (every live
creature plus the waves still to spawn) and `pullCohortIncomingDps` (the cohort
a lair hit can aggro — see below).

**The pull cohort is a *future* field, not the present one.** This is the
subtlety that makes the timing matter: the settle rule guarantees the live field
is **zero** when the bot hits the lair, so sizing the cohort from the field at
that instant yields zero and would declare every lair safe to pull. The hit
schedules the next wave, and `LairAggroTask` fires **4 s** later against
whatever exists by then — creature spawns land at
`random(SPAWN_TIME_MAX - SPAWN_TIME_MIN) + SPAWN_TIME_MIN`, so part or all of a
wave materialises inside that window.

**And it may be more than one wave.** The boundaries are condition thresholds,
not a queue: a concentrated burst can carry `conditionDamage` from zero past
`maxCondition / 2` inside a single 30-second aggro window, advancing **1→2→3**,
and the wave-2 spawns then add their own self-damage on top
(`spawnLairMobile`'s trailing `inflictDamage`, §"further mechanics"). The
delayed `LairAggroTask` scans **all** living spawns when it finally runs, so it
can find both remaining waves. Bounding the burst to one boundary was considered
and rejected: the lair's self-damage is not the bot's to withhold, so the bound
would not hold.

`pullCohortIncomingDps` is therefore computed over **every remaining non-boss
wave that can still materialise**, defaulting to all of them, priced at the
cached first-complete-wave mean. This is deliberately the conservative
direction — over-pricing costs a mission declined as `unviable`, under-pricing
costs a dead novice. It is zero only when no partial-pull path remains at all —
at `spawnNumber >= 3`, where DAMAGERECEIVED stops scheduling
`checkForNewSpawns`.

**New pure helper** (`SimBotLairTactics.h`, header-only like
`SimBotAbilityTiers.h`) takes those resolved scalars — both DPS figures, the
bot's current and maximum HAM, its damage output, remaining lair condition, boss
expectation — and returns
`SimBotLairTacticPlan { tactic, fullFieldIncomingDps, pullCohortIncomingDps, survivalSeconds, fieldClearSeconds, expectedFieldSize, bossExpected, reason }`.
Keeping it free of engine types is what makes it unit-testable.

**Selection rule — three outcomes, not two.**

- **`aoe`** requires *all* of: an installed area ability
  (`CombatQueueCommand::isAreaAction()` over the F_0.9.7 `skillDerivedAttackMap`),
  `survivalSeconds >= fieldClearSeconds * safetyFactor`, and current HAM above
  the configured engage fraction.
- **`pull`** when the bot cannot tank the whole field but *can* survive the
  **pull cohort** defined above — every remaining non-boss wave, not the present
  field (which the settle rule has just emptied). Sizing
  it correctly matters twice over: `doAggro` rolls `System::random(100) < 50`
  **independently per creature**, so the outcome is binomial and **every
  creature can aggro**. Half is the *mean*, not the worst case, and on the small
  fields a real wave produces (2–5 creatures) exceeding half is common — a
  `ceil(N/2)` bound would be wrong often, not rarely. So the default is **all
  remaining non-boss waves at full size** (`pullCohortPercentile = 100`), with
  the percentile exposed as a Lua knob for an owner who decides the worst case is
  too conservative. Pull's advantage over AoE is then not a smaller cohort but
  *control of when the cohort happens*: outside a lair-damage window the bot
  chooses its fights one at a time, and the cohort is only risked on the bursts
  it elects to take.
- **`unviable`** otherwise. **Pull discipline cannot mitigate an
  `allAttack = true` boss pull**, so a lair whose priced boss wave exceeds
  survivability is `unviable` no matter how good the pull discipline is. An
  `unviable` verdict does not fall through to `pull`; it takes the
  `LAIR_ABORT` exit (§3) — rebuff and reassess, or abandon the offer and count
  it. Treating "not safe to AoE" as "therefore pull" is exactly how a novice
  dies to a boss.

The plan is **recomputed at each wave boundary and on boss detection**, not once
per mission: buffs expire, HAM drops, and a boss changes the arithmetic
categorically.

### 3. The lair tactic sub-state machine

**Files**: `SimHunterController.{h,cpp}`

**Current state**: the HUNTING branch (`SimHunterController.cpp:3780-3819`)
scans for a target; if none is found and `lairEngageAfterFieldClear` is on, it
sets `ENGAGING_LAIR` and calls `engageMissionLair()` (`:4297`), which starts
combat on the lair as a `TangibleObject` defender.

**New sub-phase** on the controller, scoped strictly to
`missionHuntOrder && missionLairOid != 0`, and **only when the gate is on** —
gate-off must remain byte-for-byte the existing behaviour at every touched
decision point (roadmap §2.5):

```
                    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
                    ↓                                          │
LAIR_WAVE_SETTLE ─→ LAIR_ASSESS ─→ LAIR_PULL ─→ LAIR_FIGHT_PULLED
       ↑                 │                                (field live)
       │                 │
       │                 ├─(field clear)→ LAIR_DAMAGE ─→ LAIR_WITHDRAW ─┐
       └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                         │
                         ├─→ LAIR_AOE_HOLD ─→ (LAIR_ASSESS)
                         │
                         └─→ LAIR_ABORT
```

**The order is settle → fight → damage.** A wave boundary means creatures are
*arriving*, not that the field is clear, so `LAIR_WAVE_SETTLE` never leads
directly to `LAIR_DAMAGE`. It waits for arrivals to finish, then hands to
`LAIR_ASSESS`, which fights the wave down; `LAIR_DAMAGE` is reachable only when
the live count is **zero**. Releasing the settle gate on "expected count
reached" and then hitting the lair would attack it with a full fresh wave
standing there — the exact opposite of the intent.

- **LAIR_ASSESS** — recompute the tactic plan. Branch to `LAIR_AOE_HOLD`,
  `LAIR_PULL` or `LAIR_ABORT`. Re-entered after every wave boundary, every
  withdrawal, and on boss detection.
- **LAIR_PULL** (low tier) — select **one** creature from the lair's field,
  preferring the nearest that is not currently in combat, engage it at weapon
  range, and fight it away from the lair. Reuses the existing
  `scanForTarget`/`engageTarget` lane; the constraint is that the controller may
  hold at most one non-defensive target. Self-defence against additional
  attackers stays F_0.9.6's `findUnwinnableEarlyGameAttacker` behaviour,
  unchanged — creatures pulled by `doAggro` arrive as additional defenders and
  are handled there, not by a new path.
- **LAIR_FIGHT_PULLED** — the existing single-target combat path. On death of the
  pulled creature, return to `LAIR_ASSESS`.
- **LAIR_WAVE_SETTLE** — waits for a wave's arrivals to **finish**, so the
  estimator has a complete sample and the field count is meaningful. A boundary
  increments `spawnNumber` **before** its mobiles exist: wave 1 spawns at
  `(j+1) * 200 ms`, but waves 2 and 3 schedule each mobile at
  `random(SPAWN_TIME_MAX - SPAWN_TIME_MIN) + SPAWN_TIME_MIN` — **1 to 10 seconds**
  apart. **The expected count is `waveSize × boundaryDelta`, not one wave.**
  A burst can advance 1→3 (§2), scheduling two waves against a single settle
  pass; a gate expecting `N` would release after `N` of `2N` creatures had
  arrived, and — because `spawnNumber` already reads 3 — the estimator would
  then treat *nothing* as unspawned and underprice the creatures still landing.
  So the gate tracks the boundary delta observed since it was entered, and **any
  further increment while settling raises the target and restarts the
  deadline**.

  **The target counts *arrivals*, not population.** Settling is not always
  entered with an empty field: a late self-damage increment can be noticed from
  `LAIR_ASSESS` while the previous wave is still alive, and then an absolute
  target of `waveSize × 1` is already satisfied by the survivors before a single
  new creature spawns — releasing immediately and reproducing the underpricing
  bug with `spawnNumber == 3`. So the gate counts observer members whose **OIDs**
  are absent from a known-set snapshot, requiring `waveSize × boundaryDelta`
  **new** arrivals. Counting OIDs rather than differencing a population figure
  also survives creatures dying mid-settle, which a subtraction would misread as
  arrivals never coming.

  **There is no snapshot instant — the known-OID set is maintained
  continuously.** Every candidate snapshot moment has the same flaw in a
  different disguise: taken at settle entry it banks creatures that landed during
  `LAIR_WITHDRAW`; taken at boundary *detection* it banks creatures that landed
  before a late detection, since a 2→3 increment during `LAIR_FIGHT_PULLED` is
  only noticed at the next `LAIR_ASSESS` (fast polling runs in `LAIR_DAMAGE` and
  `LAIR_WAVE_SETTLE` only). In both cases the arrival target becomes unreachable
  and the 11-second deadline turns into the normal path instead of the exception.

  So the controller keeps a `knownLairMemberOids` set for the whole lifetime of
  the lair sub-machine, refreshed on **every** poll and assessment: an observer
  member is an *arrival* the first time its OID is seen, whenever that happens,
  and the boundary handler consumes the arrivals already banked rather than
  resetting a baseline. This is snapshot-free and therefore immune to detection
  latency — the property none of the point-in-time variants had. The set is
  cleared with the rest of the lair state at the sub-machine's single exit choke
  point.

  Release on the arrival count reaching that target, **or** on
  `waveSettleMaxMs` (default 11 000 ms, one second past `SPAWN_TIME_MAX`) since
  the **most recent** increment, whichever comes first; the timeout keeps a
  partially-failed spawn from wedging the bot. It always hands to
  `LAIR_ASSESS`, **never** straight to `LAIR_DAMAGE`.
- **LAIR_DAMAGE** — reachable only from `LAIR_ASSESS` with the live field count
  at **zero**. Attack the lair in a **concentrated burst** (the pull is
  rate-limited to once per 30 s, so subsequent hits in the window are free),
  watching for a boundary.
- **LAIR_WITHDRAW** — on a wave boundary, move out to `withdrawRadiusMeters` so
  the incoming partial pull's arrivals are **spread over travel time**, then go
  to `LAIR_WAVE_SETTLE`. This neither avoids the pull (`doAggro` has no distance
  test) nor serialises it; it lowers peak concurrency, and the telemetry is
  named accordingly.
- **LAIR_AOE_HOLD** (high tier) — hold position within the lair's spawn radius,
  keep the lair as the combat target so waves keep being forced out, and let the
  F_0.9.7 ability selector choose area attacks. Exits to `LAIR_ASSESS` on a HAM
  drop below the abort fraction, so a bot that misjudged can fall back rather
  than die.
- **LAIR_ABORT** — the `unviable` exit, with **two distinct behaviours split on
  whether the bot is already committed**:
  - **Cold abort** — first `unviable` verdict on an assessment that happens
    **before any lair damage and with no live defenders**. Only here may the bot
    leave: it keeps the offer, the lair record and an `unviableAttempts` counter,
    sets the continuation to `REASSESS_LAIR`, takes the buff trip, and on return
    re-enters `LAIR_ASSESS` against that same still-live lair. A second
    `unviable` verdict, or no obtainable buff, abandons with reason
    `lair_unviable`.
  - **Hot abort** — any `unviable` verdict reached mid-fight: boss detection, the
    `LAIR_AOE_HOLD` HAM abort, or any state with live defenders or a lair hit
    already landed. These **abandon immediately** (`lair_unviable_committed`) and
    fall back to the existing flee/retreat behaviour. Walking away to rebuff here
    would be unsafe for a reason established above: a `LairAggroTask` already
    scheduled ignores distance, so the cohort re-engages the bot *during* the
    buff trip, and clearing the lair target does not cancel it. Making the
    rebuff path conditional on being uncommitted is cheaper and safer than adding
    defender/aggro-task cleanup, which this chunk does not attempt.

  The split is why `unviable` is a distinct verdict rather than collapsing into
  `pull` — and why it is not a single uniform exit either.

**Wave-boundary detection.** `PveHuntLair.waveObserver` is already retained as a
`ManagedReference<LairObserver*>` (`SimPlayerManager.h:755`) precisely so the
dashboard can read `getSpawnNumber()`. That getter reads an `AtomicInteger` and
takes no lock, so it can be polled cheaply. The controller records the spawn
number on entering `LAIR_DAMAGE` and transitions to `LAIR_WITHDRAW` on any
increment.

**Boss detection needs a second signal.** `checkForBossSpawn` increments
`bossesSpawned`, **not** `spawnNumber`, so the boss wave is invisible to a
`getSpawnNumber()` watch — and it is the one full-field pull in the system. The
`bossesSpawned` `AtomicInteger` exists (`LairObserver.idl:42`) but has **no
accessor**. Add one read-only getter, `getBossesSpawned()`, alongside the
existing `getSpawnNumber()` (`LairObserver.idl:99`). This is additive and
changes no behaviour, but it does mean the claim "`LairObserver` is not
modified" is narrowed to: **no behavioural change; one additive read-only
accessor.** The controller watches it on the same poll as `spawnNumber` and
treats an increment as an immediate `LAIR_ASSESS`.

The cadence matters and is the tightest timing constraint in this plan.
`pveHunterActiveTickSeconds` is **2 s** (`SimPlayerManager.h:2578`) and the wave
check is scheduled **1 s** after the damaging hit. `LAIR_DAMAGE` and
`LAIR_WAVE_SETTLE` — and only those — schedule their next tick at
`lairTactics.waveWatchMs` (default 400 ms), so a boundary is seen promptly
enough for the burst to stop and the settle gate to start on time. The fast
cadence is bounded to two sub-phases of one gated activity; it is not a global
tick change.

A boundary must also be recognised when the bot is *not* the cause: waves 2 and 3
self-damage the lair, so `LAIR_ASSESS` and `LAIR_WAVE_SETTLE` re-read both
counters on entry and treat an unexpected increment exactly like an observed one.

**Locking**: every lair read follows `engageMissionLair`'s existing choreography
— resolve through `ZoneServer::getObject`, hold a `ManagedReference`, take
`Locker agentLock(agent)` for the combat call, and snapshot values for
diagnostics **before** releasing so `MissionDiagLog` file I/O never happens under
the actor lock (the comment at `SimHunterController.cpp:4338` states this rule
explicitly).

### 4. Offer danger filtering

**File**: `SimPlayerManager.cpp` (`selectPveBotMissionLairSpawn` /
`generatePveBotMissionOffers`)

Below `lairTactics.dangerFilterMaxTier`, reject a candidate lair whose
`LairTemplate::getWeightedMobiles()` contains any creature template that is
either **ranged** or **aggressive-pack**:

- *Ranged*: `getPrimaryWeapon()` / `getDefaultWeapon()` name a weapon **group**,
  not a single weapon, and a group may mix ranges. So the check **expands every
  realizable alternative** in both the primary and default groups and rejects the
  lair if **any** alternative resolves to a `SharedWeaponObjectTemplate` whose
  max range exceeds `dangerFilterMeleeRangeMeters` — the creature may spawn with
  any member, so the worst member is the one that matters. Three cases are
  called out because they are where an "obvious" implementation goes wrong: an
  **empty** primary/default (the creature is unarmed → melee, not ranged); a
  name that is already a direct `.iff` weapon path rather than a group (resolve
  it directly); and a group that resolves to **nothing** (treated as **ranged**
  — fail closed, since an unknown weapon on a novice's first mission is the
  expensive direction to guess wrong).
- *Aggressive-pack*: `CreatureTemplate::isPack()` (`CreatureTemplate.h:216`),
  matching the F_0.9.6 finding that `notifyPackMobs` makes PACK — and only PACK —
  the group-aggro mechanic.

Rejections are counted per reason and published, so an empty board is
attributable to the filter rather than looking like a broken mission system
(§5c.4 warns that mission health is genuinely unknown and this chunk may have to
absorb defects).

### 5. Graduation, the mission activity, and the credit sink

**Files**: `SimPlayerManager.{h,cpp}`, `SimHunterController.{h,cpp}`,
`sim_player_manager.lua`

**Arbiter** (`scorePlayerBotActivity`, `SimPlayerManager.cpp:11375`): today it
returns `grind` or `hunt`. Add a third, `mission`, chosen when the identity is
graduated **and** all three of `lairTacticsEnabled`, `isPveMissionHuntEnabled()`
and `isPveMissionBoardEnabled()` hold. The last two are **prerequisites, not
decorations**: `missionHuntOrder` is derived from the mission-hunt gate at
`SimHunterController.cpp:407`, so dispatching a `missionOnly` order while that
gate is off produces a bot that takes an order and then runs the ordinary hunt
path with no mission at all. The arbiter and `dispatchPveBotMissionOrders` both
test the same three, and the dashboard publishes which one blocked so an idle
graduated bot is attributable in one read. The important property is that
`mission` is **not demand-gated**: a graduated bot takes missions because it
wants credits and XP, exactly as F_0.9.6 let a novice grind without a market
order. `hunt` (the demand-driven market loop) remains unchanged and still wins
when the matchmaker has an order, so this adds a floor rather than replacing the
existing dispatch.

`SimBotActivityDecision`'s comment ("the arbiter deliberately has only these two
activities", `SimPlayerManager.h:368`) is updated in the same edit — a stale
invariant comment is worse than none.

**An activity label does not dispatch anything.** `runPveHunterMatchmaker`
(`SimPlayerManager.cpp:20337`) returns early when no family has demand pressure,
and `SimHunterController::startOrder` (`:370`) requires a fully populated
`PveHuntOrder` *and* a `PveHuntSpecies`. Marking an identity `mission` therefore
changes nothing on its own. This chunk adds an explicit **mission-dispatch
pass**, `dispatchPveBotMissionOrders(nowMs)`, called from the PvE maintenance
tick **immediately after** `runPveHunterMatchmaker` (`:21976`) so the
demand-driven matchmaker keeps first claim on every identity:

- Select roster identities whose arbiter decision is `mission`, that are
  hunt-eligible, that have a live body, and that hold **no active order**.
- Build a `PveHuntOrder` with the bot's home planet/city, `quota = 1`, and
  **no** market fields (`marketFamily`, `requestedResourceType` and the
  family-yield fields stay empty) — this order exists to take missions, not to
  fill a demand signal, so nothing may reserve inbound supply against it.
- **Mark it with a new `PveHuntOrder` field, `missionOnly` (default `false`).**
  Note that the controller's existing `missionHuntOrder` is **not** a per-order
  field: `startOrder` derives it from the global gate
  (`SimHunterController.cpp:407`, `isPveMissionHuntEnabled()`). That derivation
  stays exactly as it is — repurposing it would silently change every existing
  demand order. `PveHuntOrder` is a plain struct
  (`SimPlayerManager.h:709` family) with a default member initializer, so every
  existing demand-driven order is `missionOnly == false` with no migration and no
  touched construction site.
- Supply the `PveHuntSpecies` that `startOrder` requires from the bot's home
  planet's configured species row, used **only** for the terminal-resolve and
  travel legs; the lair comes from the mission board, not from the species.
- Bound it: at most `lairTactics.maxConcurrentMissionBots` dispatches per tick
  and overall, and never below the existing `pveMaxHunters` population rules.

Harvest crediting (`recordPveHunterHarvest`) stays on demand-driven orders only,
so a mission-only bot earns XP and credits without injecting phantom supply into
the acquisition ledger — the shadow-mirror invariant of roadmap §4.

**Post-mission "what next"**, evaluated in `onPveBotMissionLairDestroyed`
(`SimHunterController.cpp:4390`) in this order:

1. **Buffs** — if a tracked buff is missing or within
   `realBuffs.reapplyThresholdSeconds` and a provider is reachable, go rebuff via
   the existing v0.8.2 path. If the buff costs credits and the bot cannot afford
   it, skip to step 2 rather than stalling.
2. **Chain** — if a second offer is held and unexpired, run it. This is the
   same-direction convenience the owner described, and
   `missionBoard.sameDirectionArcDegrees = 60` already biases the pair that way.
3. **Train** — if a box on the plan is now affordable, train it. Training already
   re-derives the F_0.9.7 ability overlay, so the next mission is fought with the
   new attack.
4. **New pair** — return to the terminal.

**The buff branch is asynchronous and currently discards the decision.** The
v0.8.2 buff trip completes through several paths — real provider success, the
`realBuffs.fallbackBuffs` synthetic path, hub return, and each failure/timeout —
and every one of them resumes by calling `beginMissionTerminalLeg()`
(`SimHunterController.cpp:903`, `:1166`, `:1186`, `:1839`, `:3514`, `:4411`).
A bot that breaks off to rebuff with a second offer still held would therefore
walk to the terminal and lose it. So the decision is stored as an explicit
controller field, `postBuffContinuation`
(`NONE | CHAIN_HELD_OFFER | TRAIN | TERMINAL`), set **before** the buff trip
begins and consumed at **one** choke point that every completion path routes
through. (`REASSESS_LAIR` is a fifth value added by **Phase 3**, which owns
`LAIR_ASSESS`; it is not part of this phase.)

**The failure exits are the harder half, and they do not go through
`beginMissionTerminalLeg` at all — they tear the order down.** **Three** were
verified, and they are three separate branches, not two:
1. `onPathFailed` runs `beginMissionCleanup(true, "buff_path_failed")` for
   `phase == BUFF_UP` (`SimHunterController.cpp:2025`).
2. The **`BUFF_UP` phase timeout** — its own 600 s budget
   (`:3338`) — falls through the timeout switch to the generic
   `else if (missionHuntOrder) beginMissionCleanup(...)` arm (`:3358-3372`).
   This is a *different* branch from the `BUFF_TRIP` one below and is easy to
   miss precisely because both are "the buff timed out".
3. The `BUFF_TRIP` phase timeout calls
   `completeOrder(true, "phase_timeout_BUFF_TRIP")` in the same switch.

All three must consult a pending continuation **before** tearing down, and
a failed buff must still run the held offer rather than losing it — a bot that
could not get buffed has not stopped being able to fight. (The failed-buff
path for `REASSESS_LAIR` is Phase 3's, added with that value.)
Adding the field without converting these exits is the defect; the conversion,
teardown paths included, is a listed task.

**The credit sink**: paying a buffer. `spendPlayerBotCredits`
(`SimPlayerManager.h:3385`) already exists with no caller. Gate:
`lairTactics.buffPaymentCredits` (default 0 = free, preserving today's
behaviour). When non-zero, the bot debits the store on a **successful** buff
application only, and a bot that cannot afford it takes the mission unbuffed
rather than blocking. Credits never touch the body's `CreditObject` (roadmap
§2.4) — this is a store-to-void debit modelling a payment, not a transfer to the
provider NPC, which would be a real economy mutation outside this chunk's
approval.

## Technical Considerations

- **Pattern Usage**: the sub-state machine mirrors the F_0.8.0
  `StructureTraversalPhase` shape (explicit enum, its own generation counter,
  entered/exited at one choke point). The estimator mirrors F_0.9.6's TTK
  scoring; the pure helper header mirrors `SimBotAbilityTiers.h`.
- **Locking & References**: no new mutex. `pveMutex` → copy → release →
  `progressionMutex` for credits, never nested (roadmap §2.6). Lair access copies
  `engageMissionLair`'s choreography exactly. `LairObserver::getSpawnNumber()` is
  an atomic read and takes no lock — that is why polling is affordable, and it is
  the reason `waveObserver` is a `ManagedReference` rather than a raw pointer.
  Per F_0.8.1, any pre-lock lifecycle guard in the controller needs a matching
  post-lock recheck.
- **IDL Impact**: `MissionManager.idl` gains four reward read accessors and
  `LairObserver.idl` gains `getBossesSpawned()` (all additive and read-only;
  `src/autogen/` regenerates, never hand-edited). Note the build fan-out:
  `LairObserver` is a widely included engine header, so this is the expensive
  recompile in the change, per the §9 note that IDL cost is build time, not diff
  size. **No `AiAgent.idl` change** —
  nothing durable goes on the body (roadmap §2.1/§2.4), and the lair sub-phase is
  controller state, not agent state.
- **C++ vs Lua Placement**: all decision logic is C++ (per-tick, combat-adjacent);
  every threshold is a Lua knob under `playerBotProgression.lairTactics`.
- **Gating & Simulation-Only**: `lairTacticsEnabled` and `awardMissionCredits`
  both default-off, both in the reference gate shape (master → harness override →
  configured flag). Credits are a real mutation, explicitly approved for roster
  PlayerBots by the P.10 mandate; the lair fight itself is real combat the hunter
  loop already performs.
- **Dashboard Visibility**: new `lairTactics` and `missionCredits` blocks plus
  the inspect-drawer fields, per §11's per-object detail endpoint pattern.
- **Live Verification Plan**: the owner's matrix — a 3xxx/4xxx brawler with and
  without buffs, a novice rifleman with and without buffs, and a full template
  with buffs — run against the live dashboard and `missiondiag.log`, asserting
  withdraw latency, single-pull discipline, tactic selection per tier, credit
  award within the formula's bounds, and a completed mission chain.
- **Edge Cases**:
  - Lair destroyed by the self-damage cascade while the bot is withdrawing
    (completion must still fire and pay).
  - Boss spawns during `LAIR_AOE_HOLD` — the one genuine full-pull path, and the
    reason `getBossesSpawned()` is watched on the same poll as `spawnNumber`.
  - A wave whose mobiles fail to spawn, so the settle gate must release on
    `waveSettleMaxMs` rather than on the expected count.
  - A 1→3 cascade inside one settle pass: two waves scheduled together, the
    target scaled to `2 × waveSize`, the deadline restarted on the second
    increment, and `spawnNumber == 3` **not** read as "nothing pending".
  - A late 2→3 increment noticed while wave-2 creatures are still alive, so the
    settle target must be measured in new arrivals rather than population.
  - A boundary detected **late** — during `LAIR_FIGHT_PULLED`, where fast polling
    does not run — after some of its wave has already spawned; those arrivals
    must still count toward the target.
  - `unviable` returned on the *first* assessment, before a single creature is
    pulled — the abort path must work from a standing start, not only mid-fight.
  - Field never clears for a reason Phase 0's probe has yet to name — the pull
    loop needs a bounded budget, not an open-ended "wait for empty" predicate.
    (Creature respawn is **not** the reason: `checkRespawn` returns early for
    destroy-mission observers.)
  - Lair healed back above half condition, un-doing the 2→3 boundary condition;
    `spawnNumber` does **not** decrement, so the observed boundary stays valid.
  - Bot dies mid-lair: body is rebuilt, `missionLairOid` must not survive into
    the new body's grind state (the F_0.9.7 sticky-flag lesson).
  - Offer expires (`maxOfferAgeSeconds = 1800`) while the bot is rebuffing.
  - Two bots sent to the same lair — `pveMissionMaxActiveLairs` and the
    per-body reservation already prevent it; assert it rather than assume it.

## Performance & Cost Impact

The only new recurring cost is the `LAIR_DAMAGE` fast tick (400 ms vs 2 s),
bounded to one sub-phase of one gated activity and to at most
`pveMissionMaxActiveLairs` bots at a time. Each poll is one atomic read plus a
distance check. The tactic estimate runs at wave boundaries (at most four times
per mission), not per tick. Gate-off adds one boolean test per existing decision
point.

With both gates off the server behaves as v0.9.7 **except** for the two Phase 0

- the `HUNTING` phase timeout, which bounds mission orders only (reasoning and

- the `adds` counting correction in `checkMissionSocialAggro`: while a mission

### HOLD-POINT AMENDMENT (2026-09-21) — Phase 3 is invalidated, not just blocked

Phase 0 ran live. **No mission reached a lair**, so the lair instrumentation
produced zero records and the 10-of-48 engage question could not even arise.
In ~4 minutes with 6 hunters: 1 `OFFERS_ACCEPT`, 9 `MISSION_CLEANUP` (**all**
`mission_terminal_unavailable`), 0 lairs revealed.

Two reproducible upstream blockers, both ahead of any tactic:

1. **Terminal unreachable at 37 m.** `TERM_RESOLVED` then `TERM_PATH_FAILED`
   from a byte-identical position, every ~30 s, 5 of 6 bots. This is the
   historical dominant failure (87 of 107 abandonments) and is now reproducible
   on demand. **Cause since confirmed**: the terminal is *outdoors*
   (`TreeEntry.parent = 0`), so the F_0.7.5 cell-entry theory is disproven. The
   hunter's hybrid mover picks navmesh mode from `agent->isInNavMesh()`, the POI
   has a navmesh, the terminal is not reachable on it, and on failure the mover
   retries the same navmesh query and then gives up — **no overland fallback
   once on-mesh**, which is exactly what P.4.2's `directOverland` gave miners.
2. **Reveal defers forever.** All 32 relocation probes fail (`p45=false`,
   `probes=32 offset=0`), repeating on a 2 s cadence for the full 900 s phase
   timeout. The relocation ring — already the documented mitigation at
   `SimPlayerManager.cpp:19899` — has **no exhaustion path**.

**Consequence.** A lair-tactics state machine cannot raise mission completion
while missions do not reach lairs. Phase 3 must NOT be written against the
current loop; the engage ratio is unobservable until bots arrive at lairs.

**Revised sequencing.** Phases 1, 2, 4 and 5 stay independently implementable.
Phase 3 is deferred behind a new **mission-loop repair** chunk addressing the two
blockers, which also gates every later P.10 chunk that needs mission traffic
(credits, loot, bazaar). Phase 0's instrumentation stays — it is what will
measure that repair.

**Also confirmed:** the demand deadlock of roadmap §5c.1 is real and current —
mission traffic only flowed after four profiles were raised 500k → 900k,
because supply sits above the shipped ceiling and pressure was 0.
