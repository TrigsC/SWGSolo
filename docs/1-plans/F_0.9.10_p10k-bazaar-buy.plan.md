**Line**: P.10 PlayerBot parity, chunk F_0.9.10 (roadmap §F_0.9.10, P.10k)
**Release version**: 0.9.12 (next free version; chunk ids and versions diverged at F_0.9.2)

**Gate**: `playerBotProgression.bazaarBuy` (default off)
**Branch**: `feat/p10k-bazaar-buy` from `miner-ai` at `de2c9d7e0c` (v0.9.11)

**Status**: RELEASED as v0.9.12 (2026-10-16)

## Overview

A roster PlayerBot that needs an item, and can afford it, buys it on the real bazaar
during the same terminal visit it already makes to sell. Each training plan carries a short
**buy list** in Lua. The bot instant-buys the cheapest matching listing on the terminal's
planet at or below a fair-value ceiling, pays from its credit store and receives the item
directly into its vault bag. The seller is paid as in any stock sale, and may be a human
player, another bot or the Market Seeder. This is the first P.10 path where bot credits
leave the store, and the first where they reach a human player's bank.

## Owner decisions (2026-10-16)

The roadmap (§F_0.9.10) required the human-listing policy before this plan. The owner
chose:

1. **Humans too, with a price ceiling.** Bots may buy human listings, bot listings and
   seeded listings alike, but never above a fair-value ceiling. Bots become real customers
   for player crafters, and players cannot farm bots with overpriced items.
2. **Need = a buy list in Lua.** Each training plan gets a short list of item templates to
   own, each with a quantity. Bought items go to the vault and stay there. Equipping
   them is a later chunk.
3. **Ceiling = loot value × markup.** This is the same anchor bots sell at
   (`junkValue × bazaar.markup`, clamped to the bazaar limits), so a bot never pays more
   than it would ask. An item with no loot value (most player-crafted items) uses the buy
   list entry's own `maxPrice`. With neither, the item is never bought.
4. **No daily cap, but a reserve.** In the owner's words: "might need a weapon, armor,
   food, if it has the credits it can buy what it needs like a player would". A bot
   always keeps `bazaar.buy.reserveCredits` in the bank for doctor and listing fees.
   **Never bids; instant buy only.**

**The rule** each candidate listing must pass:

- it is an instant-sale bazaar listing (`FORSALE`, not an auction, not expired) on the
  terminal's planet;
- its seller is not this bot's own bag, and its template is on the bot's buy list with
  unmet quantity;
- `price ≤ ceiling`, where `ceiling = clamp(junkValue × markup)` if `junkValue > 0`,
  else `entry.maxPrice` if it is greater than 0, else the listing is skipped;
- `price ≤ bank − reserved credits − reserveCredits`.

Among the passing listings for one need, the cheapest wins; a tie goes to the earliest
expiry. `price` is the listing's stock price, city sales tax included, which is what any
buyer pays.

**The limiter is need, not money.** A bot owns at most `quantity` of each buy-list
template, and a bought item satisfies that need. A need-listed item, bought or looted, is
**never** offered by SELL, so a bot never buys and resells the same thing.

## Problem Statement

- **SELL closed only half of the market.** Bots list loot (F_0.9.9), but nothing a bot
  owns is ever bought, so player crafters get no customers and bot credits pile up with
  no sink but doctor and listing fees.
- **Stock `doInstantBuy` cannot be used by a bot.** It debits
  `player->subtractBankCredits` on the buyer's `CreatureObject`
  (`AuctionManagerImplementation.cpp:1198`). A bot's credits live in the MySQL store,
  and the roadmap forbids "fund the body and reconcile after".
- **Stock retrieval cannot be used either.** It requires the buyer's OID to be a player
  in range of the terminal, with an inventory (`checkRetrieve`, `:1594`). A bot's
  holdings live in its vault bag.
- **Stock resolves the seller by name** (`pman->getPlayer(item->getOwnerName())`). For a
  bot buyer this is unnecessary and unsafe: the owner OID on the `AuctionItem` is
  authoritative and unambiguous.
- **Two stores, no transaction.** The bot's debit lands in MySQL, while the item moving
  into the bag and the seller's credit land in the object store (BerkeleyDB, saved every
  5 minutes). A crash between them must never give a bot a free item, and never lose a
  bot's credits for nothing.

## Solution Architecture

### The flow

1. **Visit.** `shouldPlayerBotVisitBazaar` returns true when the bot has a sell candidate
   (unchanged) **or** an unmet, affordable need. It checks in-memory rows and config only,
   never `AuctionsMap`.
2. **At the terminal, choose.** After listing, the controller calls
   `buyPlayerBotItemsAtTerminal`, which:
   - scans the planet's bazaar listings, prefiltering by item type and price;
   - loads only the surviving candidates' objects to read their template and
     `junkValue`;
   - picks at most `maxPurchasesPerVisit` listings.
3. **Debit first (durable before the object store moves).** For each choice, under
   `progressionMutex`:
   - debit the bank in memory and append a `credits_bank` ledger row with
     `source = bazaar_buy`, `ref_id = auction OID` and `detail = obj=<item OID>`;
   - record an in-memory `PlayerBotBazaarPurchase` in state `DEBITED`;
   - set `progressionBazaarFlushRequested` and call `kickPveMaintenanceNow()`, the
     F_0.9.9 instant-pickup kick.

   The bot's visit then ends. It never waits at the terminal.
4. **Lane: flush, then execute.** The maintenance lane flushes. For each `DEBITED`
   purchase whose debit row is **applied** (at or below the identity's
   `bank_ledger_applied_id`), it calls the new native
   `AuctionManager::buySimBotItem`. That call, under the stock locks:
   - rechecks the listing;
   - pays the seller;
   - moves the item straight into the buyer's bag;
   - retires the `AuctionItem`.
5. **Lane: commit or refund.**
   - **Success:** the item gets a `held` row (`source = bazaar_buy`, `ref_id` = auction)
     and the purchase moves to `COMMITTED`.
   - **Any refusal** (listing gone, sold to someone else, expired, seller unresolvable,
     transfer failed) appends a `bazaar_buy_refund` row for `+price`, and the purchase
     moves to `REFUNDED`.

   Then the lane flushes again with force.
6. **Bot-to-bot.** When the seller is another bot's bag, `buySimBotItem` enqueues the
   F_0.9.9 sale POD (`enqueuePlayerBotBazaarSale`) exactly as `doInstantBuy` does, with
   the buyer bag as `buyerId`. The existing settlement pays the seller bot in the same
   lane pass.

### Why debit first

The order is chosen so that every crash leaves a state boot can resolve from evidence.
Both cases below have the debit applied:

| Crash point | MySQL | Object store after reboot | Boot resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before the debit flush | nothing | unchanged (buy not executed) | nothing to do; the row was never written or is voided above the mark |
| After the debit, before execution | applied | listing still `FORSALE` | **refund** |
| After execution, before the next 5-minute save | applied | reverted: listing `FORSALE`, item with seller, seller not paid | **refund** (consistent: nobody paid, nobody delivered) |
| After execution and the 5-minute save | applied | item in buyer's bag, seller paid | **commit** (adopt the held row if it was not flushed) |

A bot can therefore never hold a bought item without an applied debit. The only
reversible case is an object store that reverts after MySQL committed, and boot refunds
it. This is the deliberate inverse of SELL: there, the player's side had to be instant,
so the bot carried the residual window. Here no player waits on the bot. The cost is a
few seconds between the visit and delivery to the vault, which no one sees.

### Paid is decided by receipts (the F_0.9.9 rule, reused)

A purchase is identified by **`(identityId, auctionOid)`**, never by the auction alone:
two bots on the same planet can debit for the same listing before one of them wins, and
the loser must keep its own refund state. Its money state is the sum of **applied**
`credits_bank` ledger rows **for that identity** with `ref_id` = that auction OID and
`source IN ('bazaar_buy','bazaar_buy_refund')`:

| Net sum | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `< 0` | debited and not refunded: delivered, or awaiting execution or resolution |
| `0` | refunded, or never debited |

The **delivery** question is answered by the object store: is the object (OID from the
debit row's `detail`) contained in this identity's bag? No purchase state column is ever
consulted for money. No new table is needed. The in-memory `PlayerBotBazaarPurchase` map,
keyed by `(identityId, auctionOid)`, is rebuilt at load from those ledger rows. The boot
reconcile resolves every net `< 0` purchase with a pure decision:

| Net debit | Object location | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| `< 0` | this identity's bag | `COMMIT` (ensure the `held` row; no new money row) |
| `< 0` | anywhere else, or missing | `REFUND` (append `bazaar_buy_refund +price`) |
| `0` | any | `DONE` |

A `REFUND` is idempotent by the same receipt sum: a second pass sees `0` and does nothing.
Rows above the applied mark are voided at load by the existing F_0.9.9 path before this
reconcile runs.

### Seller payment: by OID, through the stock code

`buySimBotItem` resolves the seller from `AuctionItem.ownerID`:

1. **A vault bag** (`AiEconomyManager::isItemBag`): seller is a bot. Payment goes
   through the sale POD and the F_0.9.9 settlement. No creature is touched.
2. **A `CreatureObject` with `isPlayerCreature()`**: a human player, online or offline (an
   offline character loads from the object store), or the Market Seeder's system seller.
   Payment uses the **same code** stock `doInstantBuy` runs for a player seller:
   - `addBankCredits(price)`;
   - city sales tax subtracted and added to the treasury;
   - the stock "Instant Sale Complete" seller mail;
   - the `INSTANTBUY` and `CITYSALESTAX` transaction logs.

   That block is **extracted** from `doInstantBuy` into one private helper both paths
   call. It is not copied.
3. **Anything else**: refuse before any mutation (`SELLER_UNRESOLVED`). This includes a
   seeded listing whose non-persistent system seller is gone after a restart; the bot is
   refunded.

**No buyer mail is sent.** A bot has no mailbox, and the stock buyer mail goes to the
bidder **name**, which can collide with a real character's (the F_0.9.9 lesson).

### Concurrency with a human buyer of the same listing

`buySimBotItem` takes the `AuctionItem` lock and rechecks `FORSALE` under it. Stock
`doInstantBuy` takes the same lock but **does not** recheck status (its caller, `buyItem`,
checks before locking). Without a fix, a human who passed `buyItem`'s check just before
the bot's purchase completed would be charged for a listing the bot already took. This
chunk adds a status recheck immediately after `doInstantBuy`'s `Locker locker(item)`: a
non-`FORSALE` item gets `PURCHASEFAILED` and no charge. That also closes the same race
between two human buyers in stock code. It is the only behaviour change to a stock player
path. It is a no-op except in the race.

### Gate-off and settlement mode

- **Gate off with a purchase still `DEBITED`:** it is **refunded**, never executed. Turning
  `bazaarBuy` off stops all spending at once.
- **Settlement mode** (master gate off, obligations open; F_0.9.9) gains one allowed
  operation, `SETTLEMENT_PURCHASE_RESOLVE`. It refunds `DEBITED` purchases and runs the
  boot `COMMIT`/`REFUND` decision. It never executes a purchase.
- `probePlayerBotBazaarObligations` also reports an open obligation for **every** applied
  net-negative `(identity, auction)` purchase sum, **unless** a `simbot_items` row owned by
  that same identity, in state `held`, with `source = bazaar_buy` and `ref_id` = that
  auction, proves the commit. "The item has no row" would be wrong: in a bot-to-bot buy,
  the seller's pre-purchase row for the same object still exists. With this test, a
  gate-off boot still resolves a purchase that crashed mid-flight.

### Bot-to-bot recovery (the seller's side)

With instant delivery there is no buyer-owned `SOLD` record for a bot buyer, so
`findBuyerOwnedBotSaleLocked` would find nothing for a lost bot-to-bot sale POD. The
seller's listing reconcile gains one row in its decision table: a `LISTED` row whose
auction is missing and whose object is in **another identity's bag**, where that identity
holds a net-negative applied `bazaar_buy` debit for the same auction, is
`RECONCILE_SETTLE_FROM_BUYER`:

- gross = the buyer's debit;
- tax = from the listing row, else 0.

Today this case is `ORPHANED`.

### One row per object: the bot-to-bot handoff

`simbot_items` is keyed by `object_id`. In a bot-to-bot trade, the seller's `listed` row
and the buyer's new `held` row are the **same row**. The existing sale settlement deletes
`progressionItems[objectId]` as the seller's row (`SimPlayerManager.cpp:12327`), which
would erase the buyer's item. So the trade is one **ownership handoff**, never a delete
followed by an insert:

- **Purchase commit**, when a row for the object already exists and is owned by another
  identity, converts it in place:
  - `identity_id` becomes the buyer;
  - state `held`, `source = bazaar_buy`, `ref_id` = auction, `ask_price = 0`;
  - `version++`, dirty;
  - the per-identity held counts move from the seller to the buyer;
  - the buyer's item ledger +1 is written.

  With no row, it inserts one as usual.
- **Sale settlement** writes the seller's item receipt (−1, `bazaar_sale`) as today. It
  runs its **whole** row-cleanup block **only if the row is still owned by the seller
  identity**:
  - the seller's held-count decrement;
  - the dirty and delete bookkeeping;
  - the row drop.

  A row already handed to the buyer is left alone, so the seller's held count is never
  decremented twice: the handoff already moved it. Without a row, the held count is read
  unchanged for the receipt's `balance_after`, as today. A pure helper decides this
  (`SimBotBazaar::settlementDropsItemRow(rowIdentity, sellerIdentity)`).
- **Order independence.** Commit runs before settlement in the same lane pass, but the
  rule holds in either order:
  - if settlement runs first, it drops the seller's row, and commit then inserts a fresh
    buyer row;
  - if commit runs first, settlement leaves the converted row alone.

  Both orders end with exactly one buyer-owned `held` row and one receipt per side.

## Implementation Details

### 1. Lua config

**File**: `MMOCoreORB/bin/scripts/managers/sim_player_manager.lua`

**Current state**: `playerBotProgression` holds `bazaarSell`, `bazaarSaleBacklogMax` and
the `bazaar` block (markup, limits, visit cooldown, sweep, `drainOnDisable`). Each
`trainingPlans` entry has `name`, `goalSkill(s)`, `profession`, `weaponTemplate`,
`weaponType`, and optionally `linePriority` and `parallelXpStreams`.

**Modifications**:

- `playerBotProgression.bazaarBuy = false`, beside `bazaarSell`.
- `bazaar.buy` sub-block:
  - `reserveCredits` (5,000; clamped 0..1,000,000);
  - `maxPurchasesPerVisit` (2; 1..10);
  - `maxPendingPerBot` (3; 1..10);
  - `candidateScanLimit` (25; 1..200; the most listing objects loaded **per need** per
    visit);
  - `maxObjectLoadsPerVisit` (100; 1..1,000; the overall cap across all needs in one
    visit, so the worst-case total is `min(maxObjectLoadsPerVisit, needs ×
    candidateScanLimit)`);
  - `executeTimeoutSeconds` (300; a `DEBITED` purchase not executable within this window,
    for example because the database is down, is refunded once the mark applies, never
    executed late).
- An optional per-plan `buyList`, a list of `{ template = "<object .iff path>",
  quantity = 1, maxPrice = 0 }`:
  - at most 8 entries per plan (extra entries are rejected with a `loadError`);
  - `quantity` is clamped 1..5;
  - `maxPrice` is clamped 0..`bazaar.maxPrice`; 0 means use loot value only.
  - Invalid templates (`TemplateManager` lookup fails) are rejected at load with a
    `loadError`, like `weaponType`.
- **Committed values:** `bazaarBuy = false`, and **every committed `buyList` empty or
  absent**. Arming production buy lists is a live-only step, like `bazaarSell`. The
  verification profile adds a scoped harness buy list.
- **Scenarios:** append the `bazaar_buy_*` scenarios to `playerBotParityTest` (§7).

### 2. `AuctionManager`: the bot buyer entry

**Files**: `MMOCoreORB/src/server/zone/managers/auction/AuctionManager.idl`,
`AuctionManagerImplementation.cpp`

**Current state**: The bot surface is `isSimBotListing`, `listSimBotItem`,
`retrieveSimBotItem` and `releaseSimBotSale` (`.idl:89-98`, `.cpp:69-208`).
`doInstantBuy` (`:1198-1427`) pays a player seller inline and sends a bot seller's sale
POD.

**Modifications**:

- **New native** `int buySimBotItem(AuctionItem item, SceneObject bag, final string
  buyerName, unsigned long expectedObjectId, int expectedPrice)` (IDL one-liner; autogen regenerates). Order of work:
  1. Validate without locks: bag is an item bag; item is `isOnBazaar()`, not an auction;
     vendor exists; `ownerID != bag`. Resolve the seller by OID (bag / player creature /
     refuse).
  2. `Locker auctionLock(item)`. Recheck `FORSALE`, not expired,
     `getAuctionedItemObjectID() == expectedObjectId` and `getPrice() == expectedPrice`. Otherwise return a distinct refusal code (`GONE`,
     `PRICE_CHANGED`, `SELLER_UNRESOLVED`, `TRANSFER_FAILED`), a small enum in the
     `.idl`'s neighbour header.
  3. Lock order exactly as `retrieveSimBotItem`: `AuctionItem` → listed object → bag.
     Transfer the object into the bag (`transferObject(object, -1, false)`). On failure,
     abort the transaction log and return `TRANSFER_FAILED` with nothing else changed.
  4. Still under the item lock, **first snapshot** everything the payment step needs:
     auction OID, auctioned object OID, the seller OID (bag or creature), gross price,
     tax, vendor and city. Then:
     - remove the seller's commodity-limit entry (`removeFromCommodityLimit` while the
       owner is still the seller);
     - set `SOLD` and then `RETRIEVED`, with buyer = bag OID and bidder = buyer name;
     - `auctionMap->deleteItem(vendor, item)`;
     - `setAuctionedItemObjectID(0)`.

     This is the same end state as a stock buy followed by a retrieve.
  5. Release **all three** locks in reverse order (bag, object, `AuctionItem`). The
     lockers are scoped to one block that ends here. Then pay the seller **from the
     snapshot only** (the `AuctionItem` is already deleted and its object OID zeroed). No
     seller, city, progression or mail lock is ever taken while a listing, object or bag
     lock is held:
     - **player creature:** call the extracted helper (below);
     - **bag:** enqueue `PlayerBotBazaarSale` with `released = true`,
       `buyerId = buyer bag OID`, gross and tax computed as `doInstantBuy` computes them,
       and add city tax to the treasury as the existing bot branch does.

     No manager mutex is held at any point.
- **Extract** the player-seller payment block of `doInstantBuy` (the `Locker
  slocker(seller)` credit, the city-tax transaction log and subtraction, and the seller
  mail) into a private helper `payInstantSaleSeller(...)`. `doInstantBuy` then calls it
  unchanged in behaviour. Instant-sale (bazaar) mail only; vendor listings are out of
  scope because a bot never buys from vendors.
- **`doInstantBuy` race fix:** immediately after `Locker locker(item)`, return
  `PURCHASEFAILED` to the buyer with no state change unless the status is `FORSALE`
  (§Concurrency).
- **`TransactionLog`** for a bot purchase: `TrxCode::INSTANTBUY` with the bag as the
  buyer side, so stock logging (when enabled) records the purchase like any other.

### 3. `SimPlayerManager`: config, gate, choice, execution, reconcile

**Files**: `MMOCoreORB/src/server/zone/objects/creature/simplayer/SimPlayerManager.{h,cpp}`

**Modifications**:

- **Config** (beside the `bazaar` block load, `:8829-8890`): parse `bazaarBuy` and
  `bazaar.buy`, and each plan's `buyList` (into `SimBotTrainingPlan`, with a resolved
  template CRC and client game-object type per entry). Include `buyList` in the plan
  signature (`:8036`) so a buy-list edit refreshes plans.
- **Gate** `playerBotBazaarBuyGateEnabled(uint64 identityId)`, in the reference shape of
  `playerBotBazaarSellGateEnabled` (`:13096`):
  1. master gate;
  2. identity-scoped harness override;
  3. configured capability;
  4. then vault available, market enabled, and neither item nor listing reconcile pending;
  5. then `progressionLoaded`.

  It is independent of `bazaarSell`: a bot may buy with selling off.
- **Need** helper `playerBotUnmetBuyNeedsLocked(identityId)`: for each buy-list entry,
  `quantity − (held or listed rows with that template CRC) − (DEBITED purchases for that
  template)`. Pure counting is in `SimBotBazaar.h`.
- **Visit trigger**: `shouldPlayerBotVisitBazaar` (`:11742`) returns true if the existing
  sell check passes **or** the buy gate is open, there is an unmet need, the bank above
  reserve is at least the entry's minimum possible price (`bazaar.minPrice`), and the
  pending purchases are below `maxPendingPerBot`. `beginPlayerBotBazaarVisit` accepts
  either gate; the sell-gate check moves inside the sell branch.
- **SELL exclusion**: the candidate filters in `shouldPlayerBotVisitBazaar` and
  `listPlayerBotItemsAtTerminal` (`:11998`) skip a held item whose template CRC matches an
  unmet-or-just-met buy-list need (that is, it counts toward `quantity`). A new counter,
  `bazaar.skipped.needed`, records the skip. The pure helper decides which items are
  reserved: the first `quantity` items per template by `acquired_at`.
- **Choice** `buyPlayerBotItemsAtTerminal(identityId, terminal)`:
  1. Snapshot needs under `progressionMutex`, then release it.
  2. Scan `getBazaarTerminalData(planet, "", nullptr)` under each list's `ReadLocker`, as
     the human-floor scan does (`:12090-12110`). Keep `FORSALE`, non-auction, unexpired
     listings that are not owned by this bot's bag, whose item type matches a need's
     game-object type, and whose price is ≤ the need's widest possible ceiling.
  3. **Per need**, sort that need's prefiltered listings ascending by price. Each need has
     its own budget of `candidateScanLimit` object loads, so a cheap non-matching
     template can never starve another need. An overall `maxObjectLoadsPerVisit` caps the
     sum, and it is shared **round-robin**: loads are granted one at a time to each unmet
     need in turn, so no need gets a second load before every other need with candidates
     left has had its next one. The starting need also rotates per identity across visits
     (`progressionBazaarBuyNeedCursor`, in memory, under `progressionMutex`), so a need
     late in the list is never permanently last. Resolve each listing's template CRC from a bounded **listing-template cache**
     (auction OID → template CRC and `junkValue`; listings are immutable once published;
     4,096 entries, oldest evicted). The cache is guarded by its own **leaf mutex**,
     `bazaarListingTemplateCacheMutex`:
     - **lookup:** take the mutex, copy the entry out, release;
     - **miss:** with no mutex held, `zoneServer->getObject`, lock the object, read the
       template and `junkValue`, unlock;
     - **insert:** take the mutex, insert or keep an existing entry (another bot may have
       raced the same miss, and both values are equal), evict the oldest above the cap,
       release.

     The cache mutex is never held across object resolution, an object lock or another
     manager mutex, and nothing is acquired under it. A listing already known not to
     match is skipped without a load, so the budget advances past it across visits.
  4. Apply the full rule (`SimBotBazaar::decidePurchase`). Keep a **working need count per
     template**, decremented on each choice, so two listings can never fill one
     `quantity = 1` need. Never choose the same auction twice in a pass.
  5. Before **every** debit, take `progressionMutex` and recheck, against **live** state:
     - the unmet quantity for that template (rows plus `DEBITED` purchases);
     - `maxPendingPerBot`;
     - no existing purchase for this `(identity, auction)`;
     - affordability against the live balance minus reservations minus reserve.

     Only then debit, append the ledger row and insert the `DEBITED` purchase. A failed
     recheck is counted (`skipped.noNeed` / `skipped.unaffordable`) and not debited.
  6. Kick the lane once.

  Each decision logs a `BAZAAR_BUY` diag event.
- **Execution** `executePlayerBotBazaarPurchases()` in the maintenance lane, called after
  the flush and **before** `settlePlayerBotBazaarSales` (so a bot-to-bot sale POD settles
  in the same pass). For each `DEBITED` purchase:
  - not applied yet, and younger than `executeTimeoutSeconds`: leave it;
  - applied, gate open and within the timeout: call `buySimBotItem` with no mutex held,
    then take `progressionMutex` to commit (the handoff or insert of §One row per object,
    `source = bazaar_buy`, `ref_id = auction`, with the item ledger +1) or refund;
  - applied, but the gate is closed or the timeout passed: refund.

  If anything committed or refunded, request a forced flush.
- **Load** (`loadPlayerBotProgressionStore`, `:11234`): read applied `bazaar_buy` and
  `bazaar_buy_refund` rows (the same `COALESCE` discipline as F_0.9.9). Build
  `progressionBazaarPurchaseReceipts`, keyed by `(identityId, auctionOid)` (net sum,
  object OID parsed from `detail`). Every net `< 0` entry becomes a `PlayerBotBazaarPurchase` in
  `RESOLVE_PENDING` and sets `progressionPurchaseReconcilePending`. The buy gate stays
  closed while it is set.
- **Ledger append** (`appendPlayerBotLedgerEntryKindLocked`, `:11028`): maintain the
  purchase receipt sums for the two new sources, keyed by `(identityId, refId)`, mirroring
  the sale receipt maintenance.
- **Sale settlement** (`settlePlayerBotBazaarSales`, `:12218`): both of its row-drop
  branches (`writeItem` and the stale-row repair) drop the row only when
  `settlementDropsItemRow(row.identityId, identityId)` holds (§One row per object).
- **Boot reconcile** `reconcilePlayerBotBazaarPurchases()`, run after
  `reconcilePlayerBotItems` (so containment and rows are settled) and before the listing
  reconcile. It applies `SimBotBazaar::decidePurchaseReconcile` and writes only what is
  missing:
  - **`COMMIT`**: ensure a buyer-owned `held` row exists: a handoff if the seller's row
    is still there, an insert if no row exists. If the item reconcile adopted it under a
    generic source, rewrite it to `bazaar_buy`.
  - **`REFUND`**: one refund row.

## Technical Considerations

- **Pattern usage:**
  - The terminal visit, gate shape, ledger receipts, applied mark, lane kick and
    settlement mode are all F_0.9.9's.
  - `buySimBotItem` is the `retrieveSimBotItem` lock choreography plus the stock buy's
    end state.
  - The seller payment is the stock block, extracted.
  - The item row path is F_0.9.3's.
  - Nothing new is invented where a precedent exists.
- **Locking and references:**
  - `buySimBotItem`: `AuctionItem` → object → bag (identical to `retrieveSimBotItem`).
    All three are released before the seller creature, the city or any progression
    state is touched (the shape of `doInstantBuy`'s
    `locker.release(); Locker slocker(seller)`).
  - The listing-template cache has a leaf mutex, held only for copy-in and copy-out
    (§3 Choice).
  - Choice reads listing lists under each `TerminalItemList`'s `ReadLocker` and objects
    under their own lock, one at a time, with no manager mutex held (as the human-floor
    scan does).
  - `progressionMutex` is taken only around in-memory debit, commit and refund, and is
    never held across an object lock or `buySimBotItem`.
  - All references are `ManagedReference`.
- **IDL impact:** one native method on `AuctionManager.idl`; `src/autogen/` regenerates. No
  persistent field changes, so no IDL version bump.
- **C++ vs Lua:** the money, lock and reconcile logic is C++. Buy lists, reserve and limits
  are Lua.
- **Gating:**
  - `bazaarBuy` ships false and committed buy lists ship empty, so a fresh checkout buys
    nothing even with the gate armed.
  - The gate is identity-scoped for the harness.
  - This chunk is the owner-approved economy mutation (roadmap P.10k; owner decisions
    above).
- **Dashboard visibility:** §6. Every refusal reason is a counter, so a bot that never buys
  is diagnosable from one read.
- **Live verification plan:**
  1. Harness matrix (§7) on a two-boot run.
  2. A production visit by a real roster bot with a live-only buy list on one plan. The
     template should be one that has bot or human listings on its planet; arm one if
     needed with `createHumanListing`.
  3. An owner in-client check: the owner lists an item that matches the live-only buy
     list at or below the ceiling. A bot buys it on its next visit. The owner's bank rises
     by `price − tax` and the stock "Instant Sale Complete" mail arrives. Read through
     REST `GET /v1/find/character/` as in F_0.9.9.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Listing sold or expired between choice and execution:** refund.
  - **City tax on the buyer side:** the buyer pays the stock price, which includes tax,
    so the ceiling is compared against that full price.
  - **A human seller rename:** the OID is unaffected.
  - **A seller who is offline:** loaded by OID, credited as stock does.
  - **A bag deleted mid-flight:** the buy is refused (`TRANSFER_FAILED`), with a refund
    if the store record still exists. A deleted identity cannot be reaped while a
    purchase is open.
  - **Factory crates:** bought as one object. The template match is on the crate's own
    template, so a buy list naming a crate's prototype will not match. Documented, not
    handled.
  - **Two needs matching the same listing:** at most one purchase per listing per pass.
  - **Two bots choosing the same listing:** each holds its own `(identity, auction)`
    purchase. The first execution wins and the second is refunded (`lostRace`).
  - **Listing-template cache staleness:** an entry cannot go stale. An auction OID is
    never reused (F_0.9.9 evidence), and the listed object's template and `junkValue`
    do not change while it is listed. `buySimBotItem` also takes the expected item OID
    and refuses (`GONE`) if the listing no longer carries it.
  - **Ledger pending cap (10,000, oldest dropped):** unchanged. A debit row dropped before
    insert is the existing F_0.9.2 accepted risk. The purchase stays `DEBITED`, never
    executes (never applied) and is refunded in memory at the timeout, which never
    reached disk.

## Performance & Cost Impact

- **Visit cost:** a visit already happens at most every 120 minutes per bot. The planet
  scan is one pass over in-memory listing vectors (the same cost as the existing human
  floor scan). At most `candidateScanLimit` object loads follow per need, bounded overall
  by `maxObjectLoadsPerVisit` (100). Buy lists hold at most 8 entries. Cache hits cost no
  load.
- **Lane cost:** at most one extra forced flush per purchasing visit, plus the
  `buySimBotItem` object work (three locks).
- **Gate trigger:** no `AuctionsMap` access from the controller's gate.

## Backward Compatibility

- No schema change; existing ledger rows are untouched. The two new `source` values fit
  `varchar(32)`.
- With `bazaarBuy` off (the shipped default), the only observable change is the
  `doInstantBuy` race recheck, which alters behaviour only for a buy of an item that is
  no longer `FORSALE`.
- The `doInstantBuy` helper extraction preserves the order of credit, tax and mail.
